---
title: Roots of unity
---

How many numbers satisfy $z^6 = 1$? Over the real numbers, just $1$ and $-1$. Over the complex numbers there are **six**, and they sit at the corners of a perfect regular hexagon. These **roots of unity** turn up all over the AMC 12, in polynomial, geometry and counting problems.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- find all solutions of $z^n = 1$ and $z^n = c$,
- picture them as a regular polygon on a circle,
- use the facts that they add to 0 and that $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$,
- simplify big powers of a root of unity.

## Where the roots live

If $z^n = 1$, then $|z|^n = 1$, so $|z| = 1$: every root is on the unit circle. Writing $z = \operatorname{cis}\theta$, De Moivre says $z^n = \operatorname{cis}(n\theta)$, which equals 1 when $n\theta$ is a multiple of $360^\circ$. So

$$
z = \operatorname{cis}\!\left(\frac{360^\circ k}{n}\right), \qquad k = 0, 1, \dots, n - 1.
$$

They are $n$ equally spaced points around the circle, starting at 1. Change $n$.

Call the first one after 1 $\omega = \operatorname{cis}\tfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. Then the roots are just $1, \omega, \omega^2, \dots, \omega^{n-1}$: each step around the circle is one more multiplication by $\omega$.

## They add up to zero

For $n \ge 2$,

$$
1 + \omega + \omega^2 + \cdots + \omega^{n-1} = 0.
$$

**Picture:** the polygon is perfectly balanced around the origin, so the arrows cancel. **Algebra:** the roots are the solutions of $z^n - 1 = 0$, and by Vieta the sum of the roots is minus the $z^{n-1}$ coefficient, which is 0.

<KeyIdea>
Factoring $z^n - 1 = (z - 1)(z^{n-1} + z^{n-2} + \cdots + z + 1)$ shows the roots other than 1 solve $z^{n-1} + \cdots + z + 1 = 0$. For $n = 3$: $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$ and $\omega^3 = 1$. Those two facts crack most cube root of unity problems.
</KeyIdea>

<Steps problem="Let $\omega \ne 1$ satisfy $\omega^3 = 1$. Compute $\omega^{2026} + \omega^{2027}$.">
<Step title="Reduce the exponents mod 3">
$2025$ is divisible by 3, so $\omega^{2026} = \omega$ and $\omega^{2027} = \omega^2$.
</Step>
<Step title="Use the key fact">
$\omega + \omega^2 = -1$, since $1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Solving $z^n = c$

The same idea works for any number on the right. If $c = R\operatorname{cis}\varphi$, the solutions of $z^n = c$ all have length $R^{1/n}$, and they form a regular $n$-gon whose first corner is at angle $\tfrac{\varphi}{n}$.

<Steps problem="Solve $z^4 = -16$.">
<Step title="Polar form">
$-16 = 16\operatorname{cis}180^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step title="Length">
$16^{1/4} = 2$.
</Step>
<Step title="Angles">
$\tfrac{180^\circ}{4} = 45^\circ$, then add $90^\circ$ each time: $45^\circ, 135^\circ, 225^\circ, 315^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step title="Answer">
$z = \pm\sqrt{2} \pm \sqrt{2}\,i$, the four corners of a square of side $2\sqrt{2}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{3\\sqrt{3}}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{3\\sqrt{3}}{2}$", "$3\\sqrt{3}$", "$6$", "$2\\sqrt{3}$"]} answer={1} explain="The six roots make a regular hexagon with side 1 (the radius). It splits into 6 equilateral triangles of side 1, each with area $\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$, for a total of $\tfrac{6\sqrt{3}}{4} = \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$.">
What is the area of the polygon whose vertices are the solutions of $z^6 = 1$?
</Check>

<Trap>
$z^n = c$ has exactly $n$ complex solutions (for $c \ne 0$), not one or two. If a problem asks how many solutions satisfy some extra condition, list all $n$ angles first.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- Solutions of $z^n = 1$: $\operatorname{cis}\tfrac{360^\circ k}{n}$, a regular $n$-gon on the unit circle.
- They are $1, \omega, \dots, \omega^{n-1}$ and add to 0.
- Cube roots: $\omega^3 = 1$, $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$; reduce exponents mod 3.
- $z^n = R\operatorname{cis}\varphi$: radius $R^{1/n}$, first angle $\varphi/n$, then steps of $360^\circ/n$.
</Recap>
