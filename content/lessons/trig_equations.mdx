---
title: Trig equations and graphs
---

A trig equation like $\sin x = \tfrac{1}{2}$ usually has **many** solutions, because sine repeats. AMC questions ask you to find them, count them in an interval, or reason about the graphs. The unit circle and the graph are your two pictures.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- solve basic equations like $\sin x = \tfrac{1}{2}$ on an interval,
- read amplitude and period from $y = A\sin(Bx)$,
- count solutions of equations like $\sin(3x) = \tfrac{1}{2}$,
- combine $a\sin x + b\cos x$ into a single wave.

## Solving $\sin x = k$

On the unit circle, $\sin x = \tfrac{1}{2}$ means height $\tfrac{1}{2}$. A horizontal line at that height hits the circle at **two** points: $x = \tfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $x = \tfrac{5\pi}{6}$. Then add any multiple of $2\pi$.

- $\sin x = k$: solutions $x$ and $\pi - x$ (mirror across the $y$ axis).
- $\cos x = k$: solutions $x$ and $-x$ (mirror across the $x$ axis).
- $\tan x = k$: one solution per $\pi$.

## Amplitude and period

For $y = A\sin(Bx)$ (or cosine):

- **amplitude** $|A|$: how high the wave rises,
- **period** $\dfrac{2\pi}{B}$: how long one full wave takes. Bigger $B$ squeezes the wave.

Change $A$ and $B$, and move the horizontal line $y = k$ to see how many times it crosses the wave on $[0, 2\pi]$.

## Counting solutions of $\sin(Bx) = k$

<Steps problem="How many solutions does $\sin(3x) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ have with $0 \le x < 2\pi$?">
<Step title="Substitute">
Let $u = 3x$. As $x$ runs over $[0, 2\pi)$, $u$ runs over $[0, 6\pi)$: three full turns.
</Step>
<Step title="Count per turn">
$\sin u = \tfrac{1}{2}$ has 2 solutions in each turn.
</Step>
<Step title="Multiply">
$3 \cdot 2 = 6$ solutions.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Factor, don't divide

<Steps problem="Solve $\sin 2x = \sin x$ for $0 \le x < 2\pi$.">
<Step>
Use $\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x$: $2\sin x\cos x - \sin x = 0$, so $\sin x(2\cos x - 1) = 0$.
</Step>
<Step title="First factor">
$\sin x = 0$: $x = 0, \pi$.
</Step>
<Step title="Second factor">
$\cos x = \tfrac{1}{2}$: $x = \tfrac{\pi}{3}, \tfrac{5\pi}{3}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Count">
Four solutions.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Trap>
Dividing both sides by $\sin x$ would lose the solutions where $\sin x = 0$. Move everything to one side and factor instead.
</Trap>

## Combining $a\sin x + b\cos x$

Any combination $a\sin x + b\cos x$ is a single wave with amplitude $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$:

$$
a\sin x + b\cos x = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\,\sin(x + \varphi)
$$

for some angle $\varphi$. So its maximum is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ and its minimum is $-\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.

<Check choices={["$1$", "$5$", "$7$", "$12$", "$25$"]} answer={1} explain="The maximum of $3\sin x + 4\cos x$ is $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$.">
What is the maximum value of $3\sin x + 4\cos x$?
</Check>

<Tip>
For counting problems, sketch the graph roughly. Count full periods in the interval, multiply by the solutions per period, then check the endpoints by hand.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- $\sin x = k$ gives $x$ and $\pi - x$; $\cos x = k$ gives $\pm x$; add multiples of the period.
- $A\sin(Bx)$: amplitude $|A|$, period $\frac{2\pi}{B}$.
- Count solutions by substituting $u = Bx$ and counting turns.
- Factor; never divide by something that might be zero.
- $a\sin x + b\cos x$ has amplitude $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.
</Recap>
