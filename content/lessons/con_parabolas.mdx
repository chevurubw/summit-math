---
title: Circles and parabolas
---

Circles and parabolas are the shapes you get from simple distance rules. A **circle** is every point at a fixed distance from a center. A **parabola** is every point equally far from a point (the **focus**) and a line (the **directrix**). On the AMC, the main skill is turning a messy equation into a clean form you can read.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- complete the square to find a circle's center and radius,
- find a parabola's vertex, focus and directrix,
- decide when a line is tangent to a curve using the discriminant,
- find where lines and curves meet.

## Circles: complete the square

A circle with center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ is

$$
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2.
$$

That's just the distance formula saying "distance from $(h, k)$ equals $r$". When an equation arrives expanded, complete the square in $x$ and in $y$.

<Steps problem="Find the center and radius of $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$.">
<Step title="Group">
$(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = 12$.
</Step>
<Step title="Complete each square">
Add $9$ and $4$ to both sides: $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$.
</Step>
<Step title="Read it off">
Center $(3, -2)$, radius $5$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$2$", "$3$", "$4$", "$5$", "$9$"]} answer={1} explain="Move the constant: $x^2 + 8x + y^2 - 2y = -8$. Add $16$ and $1$ to both sides: $(x + 4)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 9$, so $r = 3$.">
What is the radius of the circle $x^2 + y^2 + 8x - 2y + 8 = 0$?
</Check>

## Parabolas: focus and directrix

The parabola $y = \dfrac{x^2}{4p}$ has its vertex at the origin, focus $(0, p)$ and directrix $y = -p$. Drag the point along the curve and watch the two distances stay equal.

For a general parabola, complete the square to get **vertex form** $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$, with vertex $(h, k)$. Then $a = \tfrac{1}{4p}$, so the focus is $\tfrac{1}{4a}$ above the vertex (below it if $a < 0$).

<Check choices={["$(0, 1)$", "$(0, 2)$", "$(0, 4)$", "$(0, 8)$", "$(2, 0)$"]} answer={1} explain="$y = \tfrac{x^2}{8}$ means $4p = 8$, so $p = 2$ and the focus is $(0, 2)$.">
What is the focus of $y = \tfrac{1}{8}x^2$?
</Check>

<Check choices={["$(3, -11)$", "$(3, 7)$", "$(-3, 61)$", "$(6, 7)$", "$(3, -2)$"]} answer={0} explain="The vertex is at $x = -\tfrac{b}{2a} = \tfrac{12}{4} = 3$, and $y = 2(9) - 36 + 7 = -11$.">
What is the vertex of $y = 2x^2 - 12x + 7$?
</Check>

## Tangent lines and the discriminant

To find where a line meets a curve, substitute and solve. If the result is a quadratic, its **discriminant** tells you how many meeting points there are: 2 if positive, 1 (tangent) if zero, 0 if negative.

<Steps problem="For what $k$ is the line $y = 2x + k$ tangent to $y = x^2$?">
<Step title="Substitute">
$x^2 = 2x + k$, so $x^2 - 2x - k = 0$.
</Step>
<Step title="Tangent means one solution">
Discriminant $4 + 4k = 0$, so $k = -1$.
</Step>
<Step title="Check">
$x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2$: they touch only at $(1, 1)$ ✓.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
"Tangent" on the AMC almost always means "set the discriminant to zero" for a line and a parabola, or "distance from center equals radius" for a line and a circle.
</KeyIdea>

<Trap>
When completing the square, whatever you add on the left you must also add on the right. Forgetting this is the number one source of wrong radii.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- Circle: $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$; complete the square to get there.
- Parabola $y = \tfrac{x^2}{4p}$: focus $(0, p)$, directrix $y = -p$.
- Vertex at $x = -\tfrac{b}{2a}$.
- Line meets curve: substitute; discriminant 0 means tangent.
</Recap>
