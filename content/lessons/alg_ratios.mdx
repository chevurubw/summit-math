title: Ratios and percents
---

Ratios and percents are two ways of saying **how big one thing is compared to another**. They look like separate topics, but on the AMC they are the same idea: multiply by the right fraction.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- split a total into parts using a ratio,
- combine two ratios that share a quantity,
- treat every percent change as multiplication,
- solve mixture problems with weighted averages.

## Ratios are parts

"Boys to girls is $4 : 5$" means: for every 4 boys there are 5 girls. Think of it as **9 equal boxes**, 4 for boys and 5 for girls.

<KeyIdea>
A ratio $a : b$ splits a total into $a + b$ equal parts. Find the size of one part, then multiply.
</KeyIdea>

### Combining two ratios

If $a : b = 2 : 3$ and $b : c = 4 : 5$, the two ratios use different sized boxes for $b$. Rescale them so $b$ is the same number in both. The least common multiple of 3 and 4 is 12:

$$
a : b = 8 : 12, \qquad b : c = 12 : 15 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15.
$$

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{2}{5}$", "$\\tfrac{8}{15}$", "$\\tfrac{3}{5}$", "$\\tfrac{5}{6}$", "$\\tfrac{15}{8}$"]} answer={1} explain="From the combined ratio $8 : 12 : 15$, $\tfrac{a}{c} = \tfrac{8}{15}$. Another way: $\tfrac{a}{c} = \tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{b}{c} = \tfrac{2}{3} \cdot \tfrac{4}{5} = \tfrac{8}{15}$.">
If $a : b = 2 : 3$ and $b : c = 4 : 5$, what is $\dfrac{a}{c}$?
</Check>

## Percent means "out of 100"

$p\%$ is just the fraction $\dfrac{p}{100}$. So "30% of 50" is $0.3 \times 50 = 15$, and "what percent of 80 is 20?" is $\tfrac{20}{80} = 0.25 = 25\%$.

### Every percent change is a multiplication

- Increase by $p\%$: multiply by $1 + \tfrac{p}{100}$. Up 20% means $\times 1.2$.
- Decrease by $p\%$: multiply by $1 - \tfrac{p}{100}$. Down 20% means $\times 0.8$.

Two changes in a row means **two multiplications**. Going up 20% then down 20% is $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$: a 4% loss, not a return to the start. Slide $p$ and watch.

<Formula title="Percent change">

$$
\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%.
$$

Always divide by the **old** value.
</Formula>

<Steps problem="A jacket's price is raised 25%, and later the new price is cut 20%. How does the final price compare to the original?">
<Step>
Turn each change into a multiplier: up 25% is $\times 1.25 = \times \tfrac{5}{4}$, down 20% is $\times 0.8 = \times \tfrac{4}{5}$.
</Step>
<Step>
Multiply: $\tfrac{5}{4} \times \tfrac{4}{5} = 1$. The final price equals the original price exactly.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$15$", "$20$", "$25$", "$30$", "$35$"]} answer={3} explain="$0.3 \times 50 = 15$, and $15 = 0.5 \times x$ gives $x = 30$. A neat shortcut: $a\%$ of $b$ always equals $b\%$ of $a$, so 30% of 50 is 50% of 30.">
30% of 50 is the same as 50% of what number?
</Check>

<Trap>
"$A$ is 25% more than $B$" does **not** mean "$B$ is 25% less than $A$". If $B = 100$ then $A = 125$, and $B$ is $\tfrac{25}{125} = 20\%$ less than $A$. Always ask: percent **of what**?
</Trap>

## Mixtures are weighted averages

Mix 10 liters of a 20% juice drink with 30 liters of a 60% juice drink. The result is not 40%, because there is more of the 60% drink.

Count the actual juice: $0.2 \times 10 + 0.6 \times 30 = 2 + 18 = 20$ liters of juice in $40$ liters total, so the mix is $\tfrac{20}{40} = 50\%$.

<KeyIdea title="The seesaw picture">
The mix lands closer to the ingredient you used more of. The distances from the mix to each ingredient are in the **opposite** ratio of the amounts. Here the amounts are $10 : 30 = 1 : 3$, and the distances are $30 : 10 = 3 : 1$ (from 20% up to 50%, and from 50% up to 60%).
</KeyIdea>

<Check choices={["$5$", "$10$", "$15$", "$20$", "$25$"]} answer={1} explain="Adding water does not change the salt: $0.3 \times 20 = 6$ liters of salt. We want those 6 liters to be 20% of the new total, so the total is $6 \div 0.2 = 30$ liters. Add $30 - 20 = 10$ liters of water.">
How many liters of water must be added to 20 liters of a 30% salt solution to make it a 20% salt solution?
</Check>

<Recap>
- A ratio $a : b$ means $a + b$ equal parts. Find one part, then scale.
- To combine $a : b$ and $b : c$, rescale so the $b$ values match.
- $p\%$ is $\tfrac{p}{100}$. Increase by $p\%$ means $\times (1 + \tfrac{p}{100})$; decrease means $\times (1 - \tfrac{p}{100})$.
- Successive changes multiply. Up and then down by the same percent is always a net loss.
- Percent change divides by the **old** value.
- Mixtures: track the amount of the ingredient, not the percents.
</Recap>
