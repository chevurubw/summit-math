title: Angles and polygons
---

**Angle chasing** means finding an unknown angle by moving step by step from angles you know, using a small set of facts. It is the first tool you reach for in almost every geometry problem. The facts are few, so learn them completely.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- use straight angles, vertical angles and parallel lines to find angles,
- explain why a triangle's angles add to $180^\circ$,
- find interior and exterior angles of any polygon,
- chase angles through isosceles triangles.

## The basic facts

- Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.
- Angles around a point add to $360^\circ$.
- **Vertical angles** (opposite each other where two lines cross) are equal.
- When a line crosses two **parallel** lines, it makes only two different angle sizes, and they add to $180^\circ$.

<KeyIdea>
With parallel lines, ask only one question about each angle: is it the **small** one or the **large** one? Equal if both are small or both large; adding to $180^\circ$ otherwise.
</KeyIdea>

## Triangles add to 180°

Draw a line through the top vertex parallel to the base. The two angles at the base reappear at the top as alternate angles, and together with the top angle they make a straight line. Drag the top vertex: the three angles change but their sum never does.

An **exterior angle** of a triangle (made by extending one side) equals the sum of the two **far** interior angles. That saves a step all the time.

### Isosceles triangles

Two equal sides means the two angles opposite them are equal. If the top angle of an isosceles triangle is $40^\circ$, the base angles are $\tfrac{180 - 40}{2} = 70^\circ$ each.

<Steps problem="In triangle $ABC$, $AB = AC$ and point $D$ on $BC$ has $AD = BD$. If $\angle BAD = 30^\circ$, find $\angle DAC$.">
<Step title="Use the isosceles triangle $ABD$">
$AD = BD$, so $\angle ABD = \angle BAD = 30^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step title="Use the isosceles triangle $ABC$">
$AB = AC$, so $\angle ACB = \angle ABC = 30^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step title="Add up triangle $ABC$">
$\angle BAC = 180 - 30 - 30 = 120^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step title="Subtract">
$\angle DAC = 120 - 30 = 90^\circ$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
Every time you see two equal segments meeting at a point, mark the two equal angles across from them **immediately**. Most AMC angle problems are a chain of isosceles triangles.
</Tip>

## Polygons

Cut an $n$ sided polygon into triangles from one corner: you get $n - 2$ triangles. So:

<Formula>

$$
\text{sum of interior angles} = (n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ
$$

The **exterior** angles of any convex polygon (one at each corner) always add to $360^\circ$: walking around the shape, you turn once completely.
</Formula>

Slide $n$ to see regular polygons.

<Trap>
The exterior angle shortcut is usually faster than the interior formula. Going from an interior angle back to $n$? Subtract from 180 to get the exterior angle, then divide 360 by it.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- Straight line $180^\circ$, full turn $360^\circ$, vertical angles equal.
- Parallel lines with a transversal make only two angle sizes, adding to $180^\circ$.
- Triangle angles add to $180^\circ$; an exterior angle equals the two far interior angles.
- Equal sides face equal angles.
- Polygon interior sum $(n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ$; exterior angles add to $360^\circ$.
</Recap>
