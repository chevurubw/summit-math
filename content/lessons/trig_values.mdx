title: Unit circle values
---

Trigonometry starts with one picture: the **unit circle**, a circle of radius 1 centered at the origin. Walk around it by an angle $\theta$, starting from the positive $x$ axis. The point you land on has coordinates

$$
(\cos\theta, \sin\theta).
$$

That's the whole definition. Every fact in this lesson falls out of it.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- convert between degrees and radians,
- give exact sine, cosine and tangent values for the special angles,
- find values in any quadrant with reference angles and signs,
- connect the unit circle to the graph of sine.

## Radians

A **radian** measures an angle by the arc it cuts off on the unit circle. A full turn is the whole circumference, $2\pi$. So

$$
360^\circ = 2\pi, \qquad 180^\circ = \pi, \qquad 1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180}.
$$

Common ones: $30^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{6}$, $45^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$, $60^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{3}$, $90^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Two facts are immediate from the picture:

- $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$, because the point is on a circle of radius 1.
- Sine and cosine are always between $-1$ and $1$.

## The special angles

The $30$-$60$-$90$ and $45$-$45$-$90$ triangles, scaled to hypotenuse 1, give exact values:

| $\theta$ | $0$ | $30^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{6}$ | $45^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$ | $60^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{3}$ | $90^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\sin\theta$ | $0$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $\cos\theta$ | $1$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ | $0$ |
| $\tan\theta$ | $0$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt{3}$ | undefined |

<Tip title="A memory trick">
Sine from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ reads $\tfrac{\sqrt{0}}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{1}}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{4}}{2}$. Cosine is the same list backwards.
</Tip>

## Any angle: reference angle plus sign

For an angle in another quadrant, find its **reference angle** (the acute angle to the $x$ axis), take the special value, then attach the sign from the quadrant.

| Quadrant | $\cos$ ($x$) | $\sin$ ($y$) | $\tan$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| II | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ |
| III | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| IV | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ |

<Steps problem="Find $\sin 225^\circ$.">
<Step title="Quadrant">
$225^\circ$ is in quadrant III, where $y$ is negative.
</Step>
<Step title="Reference angle">
$225^\circ - 180^\circ = 45^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step title="Combine">
$\sin 225^\circ = -\sin 45^\circ = -\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The other three functions

$$
\sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}, \qquad \cot\theta = \frac{1}{\tan\theta} = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}.
$$

<Trap>
$\sin^{-1} x$ means the **inverse** sine (arcsin), not $\frac{1}{\sin x}$. The reciprocal is $\csc x$. But $\sin^2 x$ does mean $(\sin x)^2$.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- On the unit circle, the point at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.
- $180^\circ = \pi$ radians.
- Memorize sine and cosine of $0, 30, 45, 60, 90$ degrees.
- Any angle: reference angle, then the quadrant's sign.
- $\tan = \frac{\sin}{\cos}$; sec, csc, cot are reciprocals.
</Recap>
