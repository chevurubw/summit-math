---
title: Geometric probability
---

Sometimes there are **infinitely many** outcomes: a random point on a segment, a random time in an hour, a dart thrown at a board. You can't count them, but you can **measure** them. Probability becomes a ratio of lengths or areas:

$$
P = \frac{\text{size of the good region}}{\text{size of the whole region}}.
$$

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- turn "a random number between $a$ and $b$" into a length ratio,
- turn **two** random numbers into a point in a square,
- shade the good region and find its area,
- solve the classic "will they meet?" problem.

## One random number: lengths

A number is chosen at random from 0 to 10. The chance it's greater than 7 is the length of $[7, 10]$ over the length of $[0, 10]$: $\tfrac{3}{10}$.

## Two random numbers: a point in a square

Pick $x$ and $y$ independently at random from $[0, 1]$. The pair $(x, y)$ is a random point in the unit square, and every region's probability is its **area** (the square has area 1).

  readouts={["P(x + y < [[s]]) = [[s <= 1 ? s^2/2 : 1 - (2-s)^2/2:4]]"]}
  caption="The shaded part of the square is where $x + y < s$. Its area is the probability. At $s = 1$ it's exactly half."
/>

<Steps problem="Two numbers are chosen at random from $[0, 1]$. What is the probability that their sum is less than $1.5$?">
<Step title="Draw the line">
$x + y = 1.5$ cuts off a small corner at the top right of the square.
</Step>
<Step title="Find the bad region">
The corner is a right triangle with legs $0.5$ and $0.5$: area $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5 = \tfrac{1}{8}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Subtract">
$1 - \tfrac{1}{8} = \tfrac{7}{8}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The meeting problem

Two friends each arrive at a café at a random time between 12:00 and 1:00. Each waits 15 minutes for the other, then leaves. What is the probability they meet?

  ]}
  readouts={["P(\\text{meet}) = 1 - (1 - [[w]])^2 = [[1 - (1-w)^2:4]]"]}
  caption="Axes: each friend's arrival time, as a fraction of the hour. They meet when $|x - y| < w$: the green band around the diagonal."
/>

<Steps problem="Solve the café problem with a 15 minute wait.">
<Step title="Set up">
Measure time in hours: $x, y \in [0, 1]$. They meet when $|x - y| < \tfrac{1}{4}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Find the miss region">
The two white corner triangles each have legs $\tfrac{3}{4}$, so together they have area $2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 = \tfrac{9}{16}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Subtract">
$P(\text{meet}) = 1 - \tfrac{9}{16} = \tfrac{7}{16}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{1}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{3}$", "$\\tfrac{4}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{5}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{2}{3}$"]} answer={3} explain="Waiting 20 minutes is $\tfrac{1}{3}$ of an hour. The miss corners have legs $\tfrac{2}{3}$, total area $\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \tfrac{4}{9}$. So $P = 1 - \tfrac{4}{9} = \tfrac{5}{9}$.">
Two friends arrive at random times between 3:00 and 4:00 and each waits 20 minutes. What is the probability they meet?
</Check>

## Darts and shapes

A dart lands at a random point of a square with side 2. The chance it lands inside the inscribed circle (radius 1) is

$$
\frac{\pi \cdot 1^2}{2^2} = \frac{\pi}{4}.
$$

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{\\pi}{16}$", "$\\tfrac{\\pi}{8}$", "$\\tfrac{\\pi}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{\\pi}{2}$"]} answer={0} explain="Within 1 of the center of a $4 \times 4$ square means inside a circle of radius 1: area $\pi$ out of $16$. So $\tfrac{\pi}{16}$.">
A point is chosen at random inside a $4 \times 4$ square. What is the probability that it is within 1 unit of the square's center?
</Check>

<KeyIdea>
Two random numbers means a square. Three random numbers means a cube. Draw the region for the condition, and if it's a weird shape, find the area of the **complement** instead.
</KeyIdea>

<Tip>
Watch the scale: if times run from 0 to 60 minutes, either use a $60 \times 60$ square and divide areas by 3600, or rescale to a unit square first. The unit square is usually cleaner.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Continuous outcomes: probability is a ratio of lengths, areas or volumes.
- One random number: a segment. Two: a square. Three: a cube.
- Shade the good region; use complements when the bad region is simpler.
- Meeting problems: the band $|x - y| < w$ has probability $1 - (1 - w)^2$.
</Recap>
