---
title: Analytic geometry in 2D and 3D
---

Analytic geometry means putting a shape on coordinates and letting algebra do the work. Some AMC geometry problems that look hard with pure geometry become routine once you place the figure on a grid. This lesson collects the formulas that make it work, in the plane and in space.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- find the distance from a point to a line,
- reflect points across lines,
- compute the area of any polygon from its vertices (the shoelace formula),
- measure distances and diagonals in 3D,
- choose coordinates that make a problem easy.

## Distance from a point to a line

For the line $Ax + By + C = 0$ and the point $(x_0, y_0)$:

$$
d = \frac{|Ax_0 + By_0 + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}.
$$

The shortest path always meets the line at a right angle. Drag the point.

## Reflections

- Across the $x$ axis: $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$.
- Across the $y$ axis: $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$.
- Across $y = x$: $(x, y) \to (y, x)$.
- Across $y = -x$: $(x, y) \to (-y, -x)$.

<Tip>
Shortest path problems ("walk from $A$ to the river, then to $B$") become straight lines after you reflect one endpoint across the river. The shortest total is the distance from the reflected point to the other endpoint.
</Tip>

A path goes from $A = (0, 4)$ to a point on the $x$ axis and then to $B = (6, 3)$. What is the shortest possible length?

## The shoelace formula

List the vertices $(x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_n, y_n)$ in order around the polygon, then

$$
\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\right|,
$$

wrapping around so that vertex $n + 1$ is vertex 1. The name comes from the criss-cross pattern of multiplications.

<Steps problem="Find the area of the triangle with vertices $(1, 2)$, $(4, 6)$, $(6, 1)$.">
<Step title="Cross products">
$1 \cdot 6 - 4 \cdot 2 = -2$, then $4 \cdot 1 - 6 \cdot 6 = -32$, then $6 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1 = 11$.
</Step>
<Step title="Add and halve">
$-2 - 32 + 11 = -23$, so the area is $\tfrac{23}{2}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$10$", "$12$", "$14$", "$15$", "$24$"]} answer={1} explain="Cross products: $0 - 0 = 0$, $4 \cdot 3 - 5 \cdot 0 = 12$, $5 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 3 = 12$, $1 \cdot 0 - 0 \cdot 3 = 0$. Sum $24$, area $12$. (It's a parallelogram with base 4 and height 3.)">
What is the area of the quadrilateral with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(4, 0)$, $(5, 3)$, $(1, 3)$ in that order?
</Check>

## Three dimensions

Distance in space just adds a third square:

$$
d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}.
$$

The **space diagonal** of an $a \times b \times c$ box is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$. It's two Pythagorean Theorems in a row: across the floor, then up.

<Check choices={["$5$", "$6$", "$7$", "$\\sqrt{11}$", "$11$"]} answer={2} explain="$\sqrt{2^2 + 3^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{49} = 7$.">
What is the distance from the origin to $(2, 3, 6)$?
</Check>

<Check choices={["$12$", "$13$", "$14$", "$15$", "$19$"]} answer={1} explain="$\sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.">
How long is the space diagonal of a $3 \times 4 \times 12$ box?
</Check>

<KeyIdea>
Choose coordinates to make the numbers small: put a right angle at the origin, a side along an axis, or a center at $(0, 0)$. A good setup is often half the solution.
</KeyIdea>

<Trap>
The shoelace formula needs the vertices **in order** around the shape. Crossed orderings give the wrong area.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- Point to line: $\dfrac{|Ax_0 + By_0 + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}$.
- Reflections swap or negate coordinates; reflect to straighten shortest paths.
- Shoelace: half the absolute value of the criss-cross sum.
- 3D distance adds a third square; box diagonal $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$.
</Recap>
