---
title: Remainder and Factor Theorems
---

Dividing polynomials by long division is slow. Usually the AMC only wants the **remainder**, and there's a shortcut that turns a whole division into a single substitution.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- find the remainder of $P(x) \div (x - a)$ instantly,
- test whether $x - a$ is a factor,
- divide quickly with synthetic division,
- find remainders when dividing by a quadratic,
- get the sum of the coefficients by plugging in 1.

## The Remainder Theorem

Dividing $P(x)$ by $x - a$ gives a quotient $Q(x)$ and a number $R$ left over:

$$
P(x) = (x - a)\,Q(x) + R.
$$

Now plug in $x = a$. The first term becomes $0 \cdot Q(a) = 0$, so

$$
P(a) = R.
$$

**The remainder when dividing by $x - a$ is just $P(a)$.** Slide $a$ along the graph of $P(x) = x^3 - 3x + 1$; the height of the point is the remainder.

## The Factor Theorem

Remainder 0 means it divides evenly:

$$
x - a \text{ is a factor of } P(x) \iff P(a) = 0.
$$

So roots and factors are the same information: $a$ is a root exactly when $x - a$ is a factor.

<Check choices={["$-1$", "$0$", "$1$", "$2$", "$5$"]} answer={2} explain="We need $P(2) = 0$: $8 + 2k - 10 = 0$, so $k = 1$.">
For what value of $k$ is $x - 2$ a factor of $x^3 + kx - 10$?
</Check>

## Synthetic division

When you do need the quotient, synthetic division is long division with only the coefficients.

<Steps problem="Divide $x^3 - 2x^2 - 5x + 6$ by $x - 3$.">
<Step title="Write the coefficients">
$1,\ -2,\ -5,\ 6$, with $3$ off to the side.
</Step>
<Step title="Bring down, multiply, add">
Bring down $1$. Then $1 \cdot 3 = 3$ and $-2 + 3 = 1$. Then $1 \cdot 3 = 3$ and $-5 + 3 = -2$. Then $-2 \cdot 3 = -6$ and $6 - 6 = 0$.
</Step>
<Step title="Read the answer">
The quotient is $x^2 + x - 2$ and the remainder is $0$. So $x^3 - 2x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 3)(x^2 + x - 2) = (x - 3)(x + 2)(x - 1)$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Dividing by a quadratic

Dividing by a degree 2 polynomial leaves a remainder of degree at most 1, so write it as $cx + d$ and plug in the roots of the divisor.

<Steps problem="$P(x)$ leaves remainder 3 when divided by $x - 1$ and remainder 7 when divided by $x + 1$. Find the remainder when $P(x)$ is divided by $x^2 - 1$.">
<Step title="Set up">
$P(x) = (x^2 - 1)Q(x) + cx + d$.
</Step>
<Step title="Plug in the roots of $x^2 - 1$">
$x = 1$: $c + d = P(1) = 3$. $x = -1$: $-c + d = P(-1) = 7$.
</Step>
<Step title="Solve">
$d = 5$, $c = -2$. The remainder is $-2x + 5$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Sums of coefficients

Plugging in $x = 1$ makes every power of $x$ equal 1, leaving just the coefficients:

$$
P(1) = \text{sum of the coefficients}, \qquad P(0) = \text{constant term}.
$$

And $P(-1)$ gives the alternating sum. Adding, $\tfrac{P(1) + P(-1)}{2}$ is the sum of the even degree coefficients.

<Check choices={["$1$", "$16$", "$81$", "$256$", "$-16$"]} answer={1} explain="Plug in $x = 1$: $(3 - 1)^4 = 2^4 = 16$. No expansion needed.">
What is the sum of the coefficients of $(3x - 1)^4$ when expanded?
</Check>

<KeyIdea>
Whenever a problem says "remainder", "factor" or "sum of coefficients", stop dividing or expanding and **plug in a clever value** instead: a root of the divisor, or 1, or 0, or $-1$.
</KeyIdea>

<Trap>
For $x + 3$, plug in $x = -3$, not $3$. The divisor $x - a$ has root $a$.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- Remainder of $P(x) \div (x - a)$ is $P(a)$.
- $x - a$ is a factor exactly when $P(a) = 0$.
- Synthetic division: bring down, multiply, add.
- Dividing by a quadratic: remainder $cx + d$; plug in the divisor's roots.
- $P(1)$ is the coefficient sum; $P(0)$ the constant.
</Recap>
