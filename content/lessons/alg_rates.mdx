title: Rates, work and motion
---

A **rate** tells you how much of something happens per unit of something else: miles per hour, pages per minute, jobs per day. AMC word problems about trains, painters, pipes and runners all come down to one tiny formula used carefully.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- set up any distance, speed and time problem,
- find average speed without falling for the classic trap,
- combine work rates when people or machines work together,
- handle meeting and catching up problems with relative speed.

## One formula, three forms

$$
\text{distance} = \text{rate} \times \text{time} \qquad d = rt
$$

Cover the one you want and the other two tell you how to get it: $r = \dfrac{d}{t}$ and $t = \dfrac{d}{r}$.

<KeyIdea>
Always check that your units match. If the speed is in miles per **hour** and the time is in **minutes**, convert first. Twenty minutes is $\tfrac{1}{3}$ of an hour, not $20$.
</KeyIdea>

<Check choices={["$15$", "$20$", "$25$", "$30$", "$45$"]} answer={0} explain="Forty five minutes is $\tfrac{3}{4}$ of an hour. Distance $= 20 \cdot \tfrac{3}{4} = 15$ miles.">
A cyclist rides at 20 miles per hour for 45 minutes. How many miles does she ride?
</Check>

## Average speed is not the average of the speeds

Average speed always means **total distance divided by total time**. That sounds obvious, but it beats a lot of students.

<Steps problem="A car drives 120 miles to a city at 60 mph and returns the same 120 miles at 40 mph. What is its average speed for the whole trip?">
<Step title="Find each time">
Going: $\tfrac{120}{60} = 2$ hours. Returning: $\tfrac{120}{40} = 3$ hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Total distance over total time">
$\dfrac{240 \text{ miles}}{5 \text{ hours}} = 48$ mph.
</Step>
<Step title="Why not 50?">
The car spends **more time** at the slow speed (3 hours versus 2), so the slow speed counts more. The answer is pulled below the simple average of 50.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Formula title="Same distance both ways">
If you go a fixed distance at speed $a$ and return at speed $b$, the average speed is

$$
\frac{2ab}{a + b}.
$$

This is called the harmonic mean. With $60$ and $40$: $\dfrac{2 \cdot 60 \cdot 40}{100} = 48$.
</Formula>

<Check choices={["$40$", "$42$", "$45$", "$48$", "$50$"]} answer={0} explain="Use $\dfrac{2ab}{a+b} = \dfrac{2 \cdot 30 \cdot 60}{90} = 40$. Or pick a distance, say 60 miles: up takes 2 hours, down takes 1 hour, so $120 \div 3 = 40$.">
A hiker climbs a hill at 30 meters per minute and comes back down the same path at 60 meters per minute. What is her average speed in meters per minute?
</Check>

<Tip title="Pick a convenient number">
When a problem never tells you the distance, make one up that divides nicely by every speed. The answer cannot depend on your choice, so choose a number that keeps the arithmetic easy.
</Tip>

## Meeting and catching up

When two things move toward each other, the gap between them shrinks by the **sum** of their speeds every hour. When one chases the other in the same direction, the gap shrinks by the **difference**.

<Steps problem="A truck leaves a depot at 40 mph. One hour later a car leaves the same depot along the same road at 60 mph. How long after the car leaves does it catch the truck?">
<Step title="Find the head start">
In its first hour alone the truck goes 40 miles. That is the gap when the car starts.
</Step>
<Step title="Find the closing speed">
They move in the same direction, so the gap closes at $60 - 40 = 20$ mph.
</Step>
<Step title="Divide">
$\dfrac{40}{20} = 2$ hours after the car leaves.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Work problems: add the rates, not the times

If Pat paints a room in 3 hours, Pat's rate is $\tfrac{1}{3}$ of a room per hour. Rates add when people work together. Times do **not** add.

<Steps problem="Pat paints a room in 3 hours. Quinn paints the same room in 6 hours. How long do they take working together?">
<Step title="Write each rate">
Pat: $\tfrac{1}{3}$ room per hour. Quinn: $\tfrac{1}{6}$ room per hour.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the rates">
Together: $\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ room per hour.
</Step>
<Step title="Flip to get the time">
One room at half a room per hour takes $2$ hours. Sanity check: it must be faster than Pat alone (3 hours), and it is.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Formula title="Two workers together">
If one worker takes $a$ hours and another takes $b$ hours, together they take

$$
\frac{ab}{a + b} \text{ hours}.
$$
</Formula>

<Check choices={["$2$", "$3$", "$4$", "$6$", "$8$"]} answer={1} explain="Rates: $\tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{12} = \tfrac{4}{12} = \tfrac{1}{3}$ tank per hour, so together they take 3 hours. The formula gives $\tfrac{4 \cdot 12}{16} = 3$ as well.">
One pipe fills a tank in 4 hours and a second pipe fills it in 12 hours. With both open, how many hours does it take?
</Check>

<Trap>
If a drain is **emptying** the tank while a pipe fills it, its rate counts as negative. A pipe that fills in 3 hours and a drain that empties in 5 hours give $\tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{1}{5} = \tfrac{2}{15}$ tank per hour, so $7.5$ hours.
</Trap>

## Mixed problems: make a table

For harder problems, make one row per person or trip and three columns: rate, time and amount. Fill in what you know, put a variable in one empty box, and use $\text{amount} = \text{rate} \times \text{time}$ in every row.

<Steps problem="A cyclist rides 30 km out and 30 km back. On the way back she is 5 km/h faster, and the return trip takes 1 hour less. How fast did she ride on the way out?">
<Step title="Build the table">

| | rate | time | amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| out | $r$ | $\tfrac{30}{r}$ | $30$ |
| back | $r + 5$ | $\tfrac{30}{r+5}$ | $30$ |

</Step>
<Step title="Use the extra fact">
The trip back takes 1 hour less: $\dfrac{30}{r} - \dfrac{30}{r + 5} = 1$.
</Step>
<Step title="Solve, or test choices">
Multiply by $r(r + 5)$: $30(r + 5) - 30r = r(r + 5)$, so $r^2 + 5r - 150 = 0$, which factors as $(r + 15)(r - 10) = 0$. Speed is positive, so $r = 10$.
</Step>
<Step title="Check">
Out: $30 \div 10 = 3$ hours. Back: $30 \div 15 = 2$ hours. One hour less ✓.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$3$", "$4$", "$5$", "$6$", "$8$"]} answer={1} explain="Let the rowing speed in still water be $r$. Downstream speed $r + 2$, upstream $r - 2$. Total time $\tfrac{12}{r+2} + \tfrac{12}{r-2} = 8$. Testing $r = 4$: $\tfrac{12}{6} + \tfrac{12}{2} = 2 + 6 = 8$ ✓.">
A boat goes 12 miles downstream and 12 miles back upstream in a total of 8 hours. The current flows at 2 mph. What is the boat's speed in still water, in mph?
</Check>

<FlipCards cards={[
  { front: "Average speed for a round trip at $a$ and $b$?", back: "$\\dfrac{2ab}{a+b}$, never the plain average $\\tfrac{a+b}{2}$ (unless $a = b$)." },
  { front: "Two people walk toward each other at $3$ and $5$ mph from 24 miles apart. When do they meet?", back: "Closing speed $8$ mph, so $24 \\div 8 = 3$ hours." },
  { front: "Workers who take 2 and 3 hours alone, together?", back: "$\\tfrac{2 \\cdot 3}{5} = 1.2$ hours." },
  { front: "Speed is in mph, time is 15 minutes. What time do you use?", back: "$\\tfrac{1}{4}$ of an hour." },
]} />

<Recap>
- $d = rt$. Match the units before you multiply.
- Average speed is total distance over total time. Same distance at $a$ and $b$ gives $\tfrac{2ab}{a+b}$.
- Moving toward each other: add speeds. Same direction: subtract speeds.
- Work: add the **rates** ($\tfrac{1}{\text{time}}$), then flip.
- For messy problems, build a rate, time, amount table.
</Recap>
