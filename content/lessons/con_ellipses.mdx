---
title: Ellipses and hyperbolas
---

Tie a loop of string around two pins, pull it tight with a pencil, and trace: you get an **ellipse**. Every point on it has the same **sum** of distances to the two pins (the **foci**). Swap "sum" for "difference" and you get a **hyperbola**. Those two sentences explain every formula in this lesson.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- read the axes and foci from an ellipse's equation,
- use the focal sum $2a$,
- find a hyperbola's foci and asymptotes,
- compute an ellipse's area.

## The ellipse

$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 \qquad (a \ge b)
$$

- It reaches $x = \pm a$ (the **major axis**, length $2a$) and $y = \pm b$ (the **minor axis**, length $2b$).
- The foci are at $(\pm c, 0)$ with $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$.
- For every point $P$ on it, $PF_1 + PF_2 = 2a$.

**Why $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$:** put $P$ at the top, $(0, b)$. Both distances to the foci are equal and add to $2a$, so each is $a$. That makes a right triangle with legs $b$ and $c$ and hypotenuse $a$.

### Area

An ellipse is a circle stretched by different amounts in each direction, so

$$
\text{Area} = \pi ab.
$$

<Check choices={["$6\\pi$", "$9\\pi$", "$12\\pi$", "$36\\pi$", "$13\\pi$"]} answer={0} explain="Divide by 36: $\tfrac{x^2}{9} + \tfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$, so $a = 3$, $b = 2$ and the area is $6\pi$.">
What is the area enclosed by $4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36$?
</Check>

## The hyperbola

$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
$$

- Two branches, with vertices at $(\pm a, 0)$.
- Foci at $(\pm c, 0)$ with $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ (plus this time).
- For every point, $|PF_1 - PF_2| = 2a$.
- Far away, it hugs the **asymptotes** $y = \pm\tfrac{b}{a}x$.

<Trap>
Ellipse: $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$. Hyperbola: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$. Also check which denominator is bigger (ellipse) or which term is positive (hyperbola); if it's the $y$ term, the shape is turned on its side and the foci are on the $y$ axis.
</Trap>

<Tip>
If an ellipse is shifted, like $\tfrac{(x - 2)^2}{9} + \tfrac{(y + 1)^2}{4} = 1$, everything moves with the center $(2, -1)$. Find $a$, $b$, $c$ as usual and add the center at the end.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Ellipse: sum of focal distances $= 2a$; $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$; area $\pi ab$.
- Hyperbola: difference of focal distances $= 2a$; $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$; asymptotes $y = \pm\tfrac{b}{a}x$.
- Shifted conics: work from the center.
</Recap>
