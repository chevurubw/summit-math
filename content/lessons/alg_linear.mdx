title: Linear equations and systems

A **linear equation** is an equation where the variable is never squared, never under a root and never multiplied by another variable. Things like $3x + 7 = 22$ or $2a - 5b = 1$. They are the most common tool on the whole AMC: almost every word problem turns into one.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- solve any one variable linear equation in a few clean moves,
- picture a linear equation as a straight line,
- solve a system of two equations two different ways,
- spot the shortcuts AMC problems are built around.

## The balance scale

Think of an equation as a balance scale. The left side and the right side weigh exactly the same. You may do **anything you like to one side, as long as you do the same thing to the other side**, and the scale stays balanced.

Your goal is to get the variable alone. You undo what was done to it, in reverse order: first undo adding or subtracting, then undo multiplying or dividing.

<Steps problem="Solve $3(x - 2) + 5 = 2x + 9$.">
<Step title="Clear the parentheses">
Distribute the 3: $3x - 6 + 5 = 2x + 9$, which tidies up to $3x - 1 = 2x + 9$.
</Step>
<Step title="Gather the variable on one side">
Subtract $2x$ from both sides: $x - 1 = 9$.
</Step>
<Step title="Get the variable alone">
Add 1 to both sides: $x = 10$.
</Step>
<Step title="Check it">
Left side: $3(10 - 2) + 5 = 29$. Right side: $2(10) + 9 = 29$. They match, so $x = 10$ is right. Checking takes ten seconds and catches most mistakes.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
Same move, both sides. Undo in reverse order. Plug your answer back in.
</KeyIdea>

### Fractions? Clear them first

When an equation has fractions, multiply **every term** by a number that every denominator divides into. The fractions disappear.

<Steps problem="Solve $\dfrac{x}{3} + \dfrac{x}{4} = 7$.">
<Step>
The denominators are 3 and 4, and both divide 12. Multiply every term by 12: $4x + 3x = 84$.
</Step>
<Step>
Combine: $7x = 84$, so $x = 12$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$2$", "$4$", "$6$", "$8$", "$10$"]} answer={2} explain="Multiply every term by 6: $3x - 2(x - 2) = 6 + 4$, so $3x - 2x + 4 = 10$, giving $x = 6$. Notice the minus sign in front of $2(x - 2)$ flips the sign of the $-2$ inside.">
Solve $\dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{x - 2}{3} = 1 + \dfrac{2}{3}$.
</Check>

<Trap>
When you multiply through by a number, multiply **every** term, including the lone numbers on the right side. And a minus sign in front of a parenthesis flips **every** sign inside it: $-(x - 2) = -x + 2$.
</Trap>

## Seeing a linear equation as a line

An equation with two variables, like $y = 2x + 1$, has infinitely many solutions: $(0, 1)$, $(1, 3)$, $(2, 5)$, and so on. Plot them all and they form a straight line. That is where the word *linear* comes from.

Every non vertical line can be written as $y = mx + b$:

- $m$ is the **slope**: how much $y$ goes up when $x$ goes up by 1.
- $b$ is the **$y$ intercept**: where the line crosses the $y$ axis.

Drag the sliders and watch what each number does.

<Formula title="Slope between two points">
The slope through $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is

$$
m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}.
$$

Parallel lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes that multiply to $-1$, like $2$ and $-\tfrac{1}{2}$.
</Formula>

## Systems: two equations, two unknowns

A **system** is two equations that must both be true at once. On a graph, each equation is a line, and the solution is the point where the lines cross. Move the second line and watch the crossing point.

  caption="The answer to a system is the one point that sits on both lines."

There are two ways to find that point without drawing.

**Substitution.** Solve one equation for one variable, then plug that into the other.

**Elimination.** Add or subtract the equations so one variable cancels.

<Steps problem="Solve the system $2x + 3y = 12$ and $4x - y = 10$.">
<Step title="Choose a plan">
The second equation has a lone $-y$, so elimination is easy: multiply the second equation by 3 to get $12x - 3y = 30$.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the equations">
$(2x + 3y) + (12x - 3y) = 12 + 30$. The $y$ terms cancel: $14x = 42$, so $x = 3$.
</Step>
<Step title="Find the other variable">
Put $x = 3$ into $4x - y = 10$: $12 - y = 10$, so $y = 2$.
</Step>
<Step title="Check both equations">
$2(3) + 3(2) = 12$ ✓ and $4(3) - 2 = 10$ ✓. The answer is $(3, 2)$.
</Step>
</Steps>

### When there is no single answer

Two lines can fail to cross at exactly one point:

| The lines are | Number of solutions | What the algebra looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| crossing | exactly one | you get $x = $ a number |
| parallel (same slope, different intercept) | none | you get something false, like $0 = 5$ |
| the same line | infinitely many | you get something always true, like $0 = 0$ |

<Check choices={["$-6$", "$-3$", "$3$", "$6$", "$9$"]} answer={3} explain="No solution means parallel lines, so the slopes match. The first line has slope 3. Dividing the second by 2 gives $y = \tfrac{k}{2}x - 2$, with slope $\tfrac{k}{2}$. Set $\tfrac{k}{2} = 3$ to get $k = 6$. The intercepts $1$ and $-2$ differ, so the lines really are parallel and not the same line.">
For what value of $k$ does the system $y = 3x + 1$ and $2y = kx - 4$ have **no** solution?
</Check>

## AMC moves that save time

AMC problems are written so that the clever path is short. Here are the moves to look for.

**1. Add or subtract the whole equations.** If a problem asks for something like $x + y$, you often never need $x$ and $y$ separately.

<Steps problem="If $3x + 5y = 23$ and $5x + 3y = 17$, what is $x + y$?">
<Step>
Notice the coefficients are swapped. Add the two equations: $8x + 8y = 40$.
</Step>
<Step>
Divide by 8: $x + y = 5$. Done, without ever finding $x$ or $y$.
</Step>
</Steps>

**2. Name the unknown, then translate word by word.** "Five more than twice a number is 17" becomes $2n + 5 = 17$. Write down what each letter means before you start.

**3. Work backwards from the choices.** The answer is one of five numbers. If setting up the equation is messy, plugging in a choice is a legal and fast way to check it. Start with choice C: if it's too big or too small, that tells you which way to go.

<Tip>
When a problem asks for an expression like $x + y$ or $2a - b$, try to get that expression directly by adding, subtracting or scaling the given equations. AMC writers love asking for exactly the thing you can get in one move.
</Tip>

<Check choices={["$2$", "$3$", "$4$", "$5$", "$6$"]} answer={1} explain="Subtract the second equation from the first: $(a + 2b) - (a - b) = 15 - 6$, so $3b = 9$ and $b = 3$. The $a$ cancels immediately.">
If $a + 2b = 15$ and $a - b = 6$, what is $b$?
</Check>

<Recap>
- Do the same thing to both sides; undo operations in reverse order.
- Clear fractions by multiplying **every** term by a common denominator.
- $y = mx + b$: $m$ is slope, $b$ is the $y$ intercept. Parallel lines share a slope; perpendicular slopes multiply to $-1$.
- Solve systems by substitution or elimination. The answer is where the lines cross.
- Same slope means no solution (or infinitely many if the lines are identical).
- Look for one move that gives the exact expression the problem asks for.
</Recap>
