---
title: Factoring and special products
---

**Factoring** means rewriting a sum as a product: $x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)$. Products are powerful because a product is zero only when a factor is zero, and products of whole numbers can only be built a few ways. A handful of special patterns show up on the AMC again and again. Learn to recognize them on sight.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- expand and recognize the special products,
- do mental arithmetic like $49 \times 51$ instantly,
- factor quadratics,
- use Simon's Favorite Factoring Trick to find integer solutions.

## Seeing $(a + b)^2$ as a picture

A square with side $a + b$ splits into four pieces: an $a$ by $a$ square, a $b$ by $b$ square, and **two** $a$ by $b$ rectangles. That is why $(a + b)^2$ has the middle term $2ab$.

<Formula title="Special products to know by heart">

| Expanded | Factored |
| --- | --- |
| $a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ | $(a + b)^2$ |
| $a^2 - 2ab + b^2$ | $(a - b)^2$ |
| $a^2 - b^2$ | $(a - b)(a + b)$ |
| $a^3 + b^3$ | $(a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$ |
| $a^3 - b^3$ | $(a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$ |

</Formula>

## Difference of squares: the star of the show

$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$ turns big subtractions into easy multiplications, and easy multiplications into easy subtractions.

- $49 \times 51 = (50 - 1)(50 + 1) = 2500 - 1 = 2499$.
- $103^2 - 97^2 = (103 - 97)(103 + 97) = 6 \times 200 = 1200$.

## Factoring quadratics

To factor $x^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers that **multiply to $c$** and **add to $b$**.

<Steps problem="Factor $x^2 - 5x - 14$.">
<Step title="List the pairs that multiply to $-14$">
$1 \cdot (-14)$, $-1 \cdot 14$, $2 \cdot (-7)$, $-2 \cdot 7$.
</Step>
<Step title="Pick the pair that adds to $-5$">
$2 + (-7) = -5$ ✓.
</Step>
<Step title="Write the factors">
$x^2 - 5x - 14 = (x + 2)(x - 7)$. Expand to check: $x^2 - 7x + 2x - 14$ ✓.
</Step>
</Steps>

When the leading coefficient is not 1, as in $6x^2 + 7x - 3$, multiply $a \cdot c = -18$ and look for two numbers that multiply to $-18$ and add to $7$: they are $9$ and $-2$. Split the middle term and group:

$$
6x^2 + 9x - 2x - 3 = 3x(2x + 3) - 1(2x + 3) = (3x - 1)(2x + 3).
$$

## Symmetric tricks: get $a^2 + b^2$ without $a$ and $b$

The identity $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ rearranges to

$$
a^2 + b^2 = (a + b)^2 - 2ab.
$$

So if you know the sum and the product, you know the sum of squares, without ever finding $a$ or $b$.

<Check choices={["$13$", "$16$", "$19$", "$22$", "$25$"]} answer={2} explain="$a^2 + b^2 = (a + b)^2 - 2ab = 25 - 6 = 19$.">
If $a + b = 5$ and $ab = 3$, what is $a^2 + b^2$?
</Check>

<Steps problem="If $x + \dfrac{1}{x} = 3$, find $x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}$.">
<Step>
Square both sides: $x^2 + 2 \cdot x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 9$.
</Step>
<Step>
The middle term is $2$, so $x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 9 - 2 = 7$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Simon's Favorite Factoring Trick

Equations like $xy + 3x + 2y = 18$ look stuck. The trick: **add a constant to both sides so the left side factors**.

<Steps problem="How many pairs of positive integers $(x, y)$ satisfy $xy + 3x + 2y = 18$?">
<Step title="Find the right constant">
$(x + 2)(y + 3) = xy + 3x + 2y + 6$. So add 6 to both sides.
</Step>
<Step title="Factor">
$(x + 2)(y + 3) = 24$.
</Step>
<Step title="List factor pairs with the right sizes">
Since $x \ge 1$ and $y \ge 1$, we need $x + 2 \ge 3$ and $y + 3 \ge 4$. Factor pairs of 24: $(3, 8)$, $(4, 6)$, $(6, 4)$ work. $(8, 3)$, $(12, 2)$ and $(24, 1)$ make $y + 3$ too small.
</Step>
<Step title="Count">
Three pairs: $(1, 5)$, $(2, 3)$, $(4, 1)$. Each checks in the original equation.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
When you see $xy$ plus some $x$ and some $y$ and the variables must be integers, reach for $(x + \text{something})(y + \text{something})$. A product of integers equal to a fixed number has only a few possibilities.
</KeyIdea>

<Trap>
Do not forget negative factor pairs when the problem allows negative integers. $(x + 2)(y + 3) = 24$ also has $(-1)(-24)$, $(-2)(-12)$ and so on.
</Trap>

<Tip>
On the AMC, an expression that looks hopeless often becomes a difference of squares or a perfect square after one rearrangement. Before expanding anything big, look for $a^2 - b^2$.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Know the five special products in both directions.
- $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$ is the most useful identity on the test.
- Factor $x^2 + bx + c$ with two numbers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$.
- $a^2 + b^2 = (a + b)^2 - 2ab$ lets you skip solving for $a$ and $b$.
- Simon's Favorite Factoring Trick: add a constant so $xy + px + qy$ becomes $(x + q)(y + p)$.
</Recap>
