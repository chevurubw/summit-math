---
title: Trig identities
---

An **identity** is an equation that's true for every angle. Trig identities let you rewrite an expression into a form that's easier to compute, or connect a value you know to one you need. You only need a handful; everything else follows from them.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- use $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ to find one function from another,
- use the angle addition formulas to find values like $\sin 75^\circ$,
- use the double angle formulas,
- recognize the "square it" trick for $\sin x + \cos x$.

## The Pythagorean identity

The point $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ lies on the unit circle, so by Pythagoras:

$$
\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1.
$$

Divide by $\cos^2\theta$ or $\sin^2\theta$ to get two cousins:

$$
1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta, \qquad 1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta.
$$

<Steps problem="If $\sin\theta = \tfrac{3}{5}$ and $\theta$ is acute, find $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.">
<Step>
$\cos^2\theta = 1 - \tfrac{9}{25} = \tfrac{16}{25}$, and $\theta$ is acute so $\cos\theta = \tfrac{4}{5}$.
</Step>
<Step>
$\tan\theta = \dfrac{3/5}{4/5} = \tfrac{3}{4}$. (It's a $3, 4, 5$ triangle in disguise.)
</Step>
</Steps>

<Trap>
$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ gives $\cos\theta = \pm\sqrt{1 - \sin^2\theta}$. The quadrant decides the sign. Don't drop the $\pm$ until you've checked.
</Trap>

## Angle addition

<Formula>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin(a \pm b) &= \sin a \cos b \pm \cos a \sin b \\
\cos(a \pm b) &= \cos a \cos b \mp \sin a \sin b \\
\tan(a \pm b) &= \frac{\tan a \pm \tan b}{1 \mp \tan a \tan b}
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice the sign **flips** in the cosine formula.
</Formula>

<Steps problem="Find the exact value of $\sin 75^\circ$.">
<Step>
$75^\circ = 45^\circ + 30^\circ$.
</Step>
<Step>
$\sin 75^\circ = \sin 45^\circ\cos 30^\circ + \cos 45^\circ\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}$.
</Step>
<Step>
$= \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{\\sqrt{6} + \\sqrt{2}}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{\\sqrt{6} - \\sqrt{2}}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{\\sqrt{3} - 1}{2}$", "$\\tfrac{\\sqrt{2}}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{4}$"]} answer={1} explain="$\cos 75^\circ = \cos 45^\circ\cos 30^\circ - \sin 45^\circ\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4} - \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$.">
What is $\cos 75^\circ$?
</Check>

## Double angle

Set $a = b$ in the addition formulas:

<Formula>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin 2\theta &= 2\sin\theta\cos\theta \\
\cos 2\theta &= \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta
\end{aligned}
$$

</Formula>

The last two forms of $\cos 2\theta$, rearranged, are the **power reducing** formulas: $\cos^2\theta = \frac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2}$ and $\sin^2\theta = \frac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{2}$.

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{6}{25}$", "$\\tfrac{12}{25}$", "$\\tfrac{7}{25}$", "$\\tfrac{24}{25}$", "$\\tfrac{6}{5}$"]} answer={3} explain="With $\sin\theta = \tfrac{3}{5}$ and $\cos\theta = \tfrac{4}{5}$: $\sin 2\theta = 2 \cdot \tfrac{3}{5} \cdot \tfrac{4}{5} = \tfrac{24}{25}$.">
If $\sin\theta = \tfrac{3}{5}$ and $\theta$ is acute, what is $\sin 2\theta$?
</Check>

## The "square it" trick

When you know $\sin x + \cos x$ (or $\sin x - \cos x$), square it:

$$
(\sin x + \cos x)^2 = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x + 2\sin x\cos x = 1 + \sin 2x.
$$

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{11}{25}$", "$\\tfrac{12}{25}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{5}$", "$\\tfrac{44}{25}$", "$\\tfrac{6}{5}$"]} answer={0} explain="$(\sin x + \cos x)^2 = 1 + \sin 2x$, so $\tfrac{36}{25} = 1 + \sin 2x$ and $\sin 2x = \tfrac{11}{25}$.">
If $\sin x + \cos x = \tfrac{6}{5}$, what is $\sin 2x$?
</Check>

<KeyIdea>
Most identity problems on the AMC are "find X given Y". Ask: which identity connects X to Y? Pythagorean for sin to cos, double angle for $\theta$ to $2\theta$, addition for sums of angles.
</KeyIdea>

<Recap>
- $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$; also $1 + \tan^2 = \sec^2$. The quadrant fixes signs.
- Addition: $\sin(a \pm b) = \sin a\cos b \pm \cos a\sin b$; $\cos(a \pm b) = \cos a\cos b \mp \sin a\sin b$.
- Double angle: $\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$; $\cos 2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$.
- $(\sin x + \cos x)^2 = 1 + \sin 2x$.
</Recap>
