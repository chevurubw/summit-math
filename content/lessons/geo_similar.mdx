title: Similar triangles
---

Two triangles are **similar** when one is a scaled copy of the other: same shape, possibly different size, maybe turned or flipped. Similarity is how you find lengths you can't measure directly, and it is behind a huge number of AMC geometry problems.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- prove two triangles are similar by spotting two equal angles,
- set up matching side ratios correctly,
- explain why areas scale by the **square** of the ratio,
- find hidden similar triangles in common figures.

## When are triangles similar?

You only need **two pairs of equal angles** (the third pair is then automatic, since angles add to $180^\circ$). This is called **AA similarity**.

If triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $DEF$ (written $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$), matching sides are in the same ratio:

$$
\frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{EF}{BC} = \frac{DF}{AC} = k.
$$

The number $k$ is the **scale factor**.

<Trap>
The order of letters matters. $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ means $A$ matches $D$, $B$ matches $E$, $C$ matches $F$. Match sides by the **angles they sit across from**, not by where they appear on the page.
</Trap>

## The parallel line picture

The most common way similar triangles appear: a line parallel to one side of a triangle cuts off a smaller triangle at the top. The angles match (corresponding angles), so the small triangle is similar to the big one. Slide the cut.

## Areas scale by $k^2$, volumes by $k^3$

If lengths are multiplied by $k$, then:

- **perimeters** (and all lengths: heights, medians, radii) multiply by $k$,
- **areas** multiply by $k^2$,
- **volumes** (for similar solids) multiply by $k^3$.

Why $k^2$? Area is base times height, and **both** got multiplied by $k$.

<Check choices={["$2$", "$4$", "$6$", "$9$", "$27$"]} answer={3} explain="Lengths scale by $3$, so areas scale by $3^2 = 9$.">
Two similar triangles have corresponding sides 4 and 12. How many times larger is the area of the bigger one?
</Check>

<Steps problem="In triangle $ABC$, point $D$ is on $AB$ and $E$ is on $AC$ with $DE \parallel BC$. If $AD = 4$, $DB = 6$ and $DE = 5$, find $BC$.">
<Step title="Find the scale factor">
$\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ with $\dfrac{AB}{AD} = \dfrac{4 + 6}{4} = \dfrac{5}{2}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Scale the matching side">
$BC = \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot DE = \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot 5 = 12.5$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Trap>
A very common mistake is to compare $AD$ with $DB$ (4 to 6). The ratio must compare a side of the small triangle with the **matching whole side** of the big triangle: $AD$ with $AB = 10$.
</Trap>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{1}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{3}$", "$\\tfrac{4}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{5}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{2}{3}$"]} answer={3} explain="The small triangle has ratio $\tfrac{2}{3}$ to the big one, so its area is $\tfrac{4}{9}$ of the whole. The trapezoid is what's left: $1 - \tfrac{4}{9} = \tfrac{5}{9}$.">
A line parallel to the base of a triangle cuts each side two thirds of the way from the top vertex. What fraction of the triangle's area is the trapezoid below the line?
</Check>

## Hidden similar triangles

Learn to see these three pictures instantly.

**1. The altitude to the hypotenuse.** In a right triangle, the altitude from the right angle splits it into two smaller triangles, both similar to the original. This gives facts like $h^2 = pq$, where $p$ and $q$ are the two pieces of the hypotenuse.

**2. The bowtie.** Two lines crossing between two parallel lines make two triangles that are similar (vertical angles plus alternate angles). In a trapezoid, the diagonals cut each other in the same ratio as the two parallel sides.

**3. Shadows and heights.** A person and a tree in the sun at the same moment make similar right triangles with their shadows.

<Steps problem="A 6 foot person casts a 4 foot shadow. At the same moment a tree casts a 30 foot shadow. How tall is the tree?">
<Step>
The sun's rays hit both at the same angle, so the two (height, shadow) right triangles are similar.
</Step>
<Step>
$\dfrac{\text{tree}}{30} = \dfrac{6}{4}$, so the tree is $45$ feet tall.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
When a problem has parallel lines, look for similar triangles. When it has a right angle with an altitude, look for **three** similar triangles. Write ratios as fractions with the small triangle on top and the big triangle on the bottom, consistently.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Two equal angles make triangles similar.
- Match sides by the angles across from them; all matching sides share one ratio $k$.
- Lengths scale by $k$, areas by $k^2$, volumes by $k^3$.
- A line parallel to a side cuts off a similar triangle; compare to the **whole** side.
- Watch for the altitude in a right triangle, the bowtie, and shadows.
</Recap>
