title: Factorials

The **factorial** $n!$ is the product of every whole number from 1 to $n$: $5! = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 120$. By agreement, $0! = 1$. Factorials grow astonishingly fast ($10! = 3{,}628{,}800$), so AMC problems never ask you to multiply them out. Instead they ask about their **prime factors**.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- simplify ratios of factorials instantly,
- count the zeros at the end of $n!$,
- find the exact power of any prime that divides $n!$,
- decide whether $n!$ is divisible by a given number.

## Ratios of factorials cancel

Since $n! = n \cdot (n - 1)!$, big factorials hold smaller ones inside them:

$$
\frac{12!}{10!} = \frac{12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10!}{10!} = 12 \cdot 11 = 132.
$$

## Trailing zeros

Each zero at the end of a number comes from a factor of $10 = 2 \cdot 5$. In $n!$ there are far more 2s than 5s, so **the number of trailing zeros equals the number of factors of 5**.

Count the 5s in $100!$:

- multiples of 5 up to 100 each give one 5: $\left\lfloor \tfrac{100}{5} \right\rfloor = 20$,
- multiples of 25 give a **second** 5: $\left\lfloor \tfrac{100}{25} \right\rfloor = 4$,
- multiples of 125 would give a third: none.

Total: $20 + 4 = 24$ trailing zeros.

<Formula title="Legendre's formula">
The exponent of the prime $p$ in $n!$ is

$$
\left\lfloor \frac{n}{p} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{n}{p^2} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{n}{p^3} \right\rfloor + \cdots
$$

where $\lfloor x \rfloor$ means "round down". Stop when $p^k > n$.
</Formula>

Don't stop after $\lfloor n/5 \rfloor$. Numbers like 25, 50, 75 contribute **two** 5s each, and 125 contributes three.

## Divisibility of factorials

$n!$ is divisible by $k$ when every prime power in $k$ fits inside $n!$.

<Steps problem="What is the smallest $n$ such that $n!$ is divisible by $1000$?">
<Step title="Factor 1000">
$1000 = 2^3 \cdot 5^3$.
</Step>
<Step title="The 5s are the bottleneck">
We need three factors of 5. They come from 5, 10 and 15.
</Step>
<Step title="Answer">
$15!$ has three 5s (and plenty of 2s), while $14!$ has only two. So $n = 15$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
For divisibility questions about $n!$, the **largest prime** in the divisor (or the prime with the highest exponent) is usually the bottleneck. Check that one first.
</KeyIdea>

</Check>

## Simplifying expressions with factorials

Factor out the smallest factorial:

$$
8! + 9! = 8!(1 + 9) = 10 \cdot 8!.
$$

This turns sums of factorials into products, which you can then factor or divide.

<Tip>
Know the small factorials by heart: $1, 1, 2, 6, 24, 120, 720, 5040, 40320$ for $0!$ through $8!$. They show up constantly in counting problems too.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- $n! = n \cdot (n-1)!$, so ratios of factorials cancel.
- Trailing zeros of $n!$ count the 5s: $\lfloor n/5 \rfloor + \lfloor n/25 \rfloor + \cdots$
- Legendre's formula gives the exponent of any prime in $n!$.
- For divisibility, the scarcest prime decides.
- Factor out the smallest factorial from sums.
</Recap>
