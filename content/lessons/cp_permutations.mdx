title: Permutations
---

A **permutation** is an arrangement where **order matters**. Lining up students, awarding gold, silver and bronze, or scrambling the letters of a word are all permutations. They're the multiplication principle applied to choices that use things up.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- count arrangements of $n$ objects with $n!$,
- count arrangements of $k$ objects chosen from $n$,
- handle repeated letters by dividing,
- handle "together", "apart" and "around a table" restrictions.

## Arranging everything: $n!$

Arrange 5 books on a shelf. The first spot has 5 choices, the second has 4 (one book is used), then 3, 2, 1:

$$
5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 5! = 120.
$$

## Arranging some: $P(n, k)$

Award gold, silver and bronze among 8 runners: $8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6 = 336$ ways. In general, choosing $k$ of $n$ in order:

<Formula>

$$
P(n, k) = n(n - 1)(n - 2)\cdots(n - k + 1) = \frac{n!}{(n - k)!}
$$

That's $k$ factors, counting down from $n$.
</Formula>

## Repeated letters: divide out the copies

How many arrangements of **BOOK**? If the two O's were different ($\text{O}_1$, $\text{O}_2$) there would be $4! = 24$. But swapping the two O's gives the same word, so every word was counted twice: $\tfrac{24}{2} = 12$.

<Formula>
Arrangements of $n$ letters where one letter repeats $a$ times, another $b$ times, and so on:

$$
\frac{n!}{a!\,b!\cdots}
$$

</Formula>

## Restrictions

**Together:** glue the objects that must stay together into one block, arrange the blocks, then arrange inside the block.

<Steps problem="Five people line up. Ana and Ben insist on standing next to each other. How many lineups?">
<Step title="Glue them">
Treat Ana and Ben as one block. Now there are 4 units to arrange: $4! = 24$ ways.
</Step>
<Step title="Arrange inside the block">
Ana then Ben, or Ben then Ana: 2 ways.
</Step>
<Step title="Multiply">
$24 \cdot 2 = 48$.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Apart:** count everything, then subtract the "together" count. Ana and Ben **not** adjacent: $120 - 48 = 72$.

**Around a round table:** rotations of the same seating count as the same. Fix one person's seat to remove the rotations, then arrange the rest: $(n - 1)!$.

<Check choices={["$24$", "$60$", "$120$", "$600$", "$720$"]} answer={2} explain="Fix one person's seat to remove rotations; the other 5 can be arranged in $5! = 120$ ways.">
In how many ways can 6 people sit around a round table, if rotations count as the same seating?
</Check>

<Trap>
Before computing, decide: are the objects distinct? Does order matter? Are rotations or reflections the same? A wrong answer to any of these questions changes the count by a big factor.
</Trap>

<Tip>
"Not next to each other" problems are almost always fastest by complement: total minus together.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Order matters: permutations. All $n$: $n!$. Choose $k$ of $n$ in order: $\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}$.
- Repeated items: divide by the factorial of each repeat count.
- Together: glue into a block. Apart: total minus together.
- Round table: $(n - 1)!$.
</Recap>
