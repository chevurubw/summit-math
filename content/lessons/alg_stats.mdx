title: Mean, median and mode
---

Three numbers summarize a list of data. The **mean** is the average. The **median** is the middle value. The **mode** is the value that appears most often. AMC statistics problems rarely ask you to just compute these; instead they hide a missing number and ask you to find it. One idea cracks almost all of them: **work with the total**.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- compute mean, median, mode and range correctly, even for even length lists,
- use "mean times count equals total" to find missing values,
- predict how adding or removing a value changes each statistic,
- build lists that meet several conditions at once.

## The three summaries

For the list $2, 4, 4, 5, 10$:

- **Mean**: $\dfrac{2 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 10}{5} = \dfrac{25}{5} = 5$.
- **Median**: sort first, then take the middle: $4$.
- **Mode**: the most frequent value: $4$.
- **Range**: largest minus smallest: $10 - 2 = 8$.

With an **even** number of values there are two middles, and the median is their average. For $1, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9$ the median is $\tfrac{4 + 7}{2} = 5.5$.

## The mean is a balance point

Picture the numbers as equal weights on a seesaw. The mean is exactly where the seesaw balances. Slide the last value and watch the mean chase it while the median barely moves.

## The total is your best friend

<Formula>

$$
\text{mean} \times \text{count} = \text{total}
$$

</Formula>

Whenever a problem gives an average, immediately turn it into a total. Totals can be added and subtracted; averages cannot.

<Steps problem="Maya's average on 5 tests is 84. What must she score on the 6th test to raise her average to 85?">
<Step title="Turn averages into totals">
Now: $5 \times 84 = 420$ points. Goal: $6 \times 85 = 510$ points.
</Step>
<Step title="Subtract">
She needs $510 - 420 = 90$.
</Step>
<Step title="Another way to see it">
To lift the average from 84 to 85, the new test must score the new average, 85, plus 1 extra point for each of the 5 old tests: $85 + 5 = 90$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$12$", "$15$", "$21$", "$24$", "$27$"]} answer={3} explain="Total of four: $4 \times 15 = 60$. Total of the other three: $3 \times 12 = 36$. The removed number is $60 - 36 = 24$.">
Four numbers have a mean of 15. When one number is removed, the other three have a mean of 12. What number was removed?
</Check>

<Check choices={["$4$", "$5$", "$5.5$", "$6$", "$7$"]} answer={2} explain="Sort: $1, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9$. Six values, so average the 3rd and 4th: $\tfrac{4 + 7}{2} = 5.5$.">
What is the median of $3, 8, 1, 9, 4, 7$?
</Check>

<Trap>
Always **sort** before finding a median. The middle of the list as written is meaningless.
</Trap>

## How changes affect each statistic

| Change to the data | Mean | Median | Mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| add the same number $c$ to every value | goes up by $c$ | goes up by $c$ | goes up by $c$ |
| multiply every value by $k$ | multiplied by $k$ | multiplied by $k$ | multiplied by $k$ |
| make the largest value even larger | goes up | no change | no change (usually) |
| add a new value equal to the mean | no change | can change | can change |

## Building a list that fits the rules

Some problems give several conditions and ask about a missing value. Write the list in order with blanks, fill in what you know, and use the total.

<Steps problem="Five positive integers have mean 5, median 5, and a unique mode of 8. What is the smallest of the five numbers?">
<Step title="Set up the sorted list">
$a \le b \le 5 \le d \le e$, and the total is $5 \times 5 = 25$.
</Step>
<Step title="Place the mode">
8 must appear at least twice and it is bigger than the median, so $d = e = 8$.
</Step>
<Step title="Use the total">
$a + b + 5 + 8 + 8 = 25$, so $a + b = 25 - 21 = 4$.
</Step>
<Step title="Respect the unique mode">
$a + b = 4$ with $a \le b$: either $(1, 3)$ or $(2, 2)$. The pair $(2, 2)$ would tie 2 with 8 as a mode, which breaks "unique mode". So the list is $1, 3, 5, 8, 8$ and the smallest number is $1$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$3$", "$4$", "$5$", "$6$", "$7$"]} answer={0} explain="Seven numbers with mean 4 have total $7 \times 4 = 28$. The six known values add to $1 + 2 + 2 + 5 + 6 + 9 = 25$, so $x = 28 - 25 = 3$.">
The seven numbers $1, 2, 2, 5, 6, 9, x$ have a mean of 4. What is $x$?
</Check>

<Tip>
When a problem says "the average of a, b, c is k", write $a + b + c = 3k$ right away and forget the word average.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Mean: total divided by count. Median: middle of the **sorted** list (average of two middles if even). Mode: most frequent. Range: max minus min.
- Turn every mean into a total: $\text{mean} \times \text{count}$.
- Extreme values move the mean a lot and the median very little.
- For "find the missing number" problems, write the sorted list with blanks and use the total.
</Recap>
