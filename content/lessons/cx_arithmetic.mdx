title: Complex number arithmetic
---

The equation $x^2 = -1$ has no real solution, so mathematicians invented one. Call it $i$, with

$$
i^2 = -1.
$$

A **complex number** is $a + bi$, where $a$ (the **real part**) and $b$ (the **imaginary part**) are ordinary real numbers. Every rule of algebra still works; you just replace $i^2$ by $-1$ whenever it appears.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- add, subtract and multiply complex numbers,
- simplify any power of $i$,
- divide using the conjugate,
- find the absolute value (the distance from 0),
- solve problems by matching real and imaginary parts.

## Complex numbers are points

Picture $a + bi$ as the point $(a, b)$ in the **complex plane**: real part across, imaginary part up. Adding complex numbers is adding arrows, tip to tail. Drag the sliders.

## Multiplying: FOIL, then $i^2 = -1$

<Steps problem="Multiply $(2 + 3i)(4 - i)$.">
<Step title="Expand">
$8 - 2i + 12i - 3i^2$.
</Step>
<Step title="Replace $i^2$">
$-3i^2 = +3$, so we get $8 + 3 + 10i = 11 + 10i$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$5 + i$", "$-1 + i$", "$5 - i$", "$2 - 3i$", "$-1 - 5i$"]} answer={0} explain="$(2 + 3i)(1 - i) = 2 - 2i + 3i - 3i^2 = 2 + i + 3 = 5 + i$.">

</Check>

## Powers of $i$ repeat every 4

$$
i^1 = i,\quad i^2 = -1,\quad i^3 = -i,\quad i^4 = 1,\quad i^5 = i,\ \dots
$$

So to simplify $i^n$, only the remainder of $n$ divided by 4 matters. Each multiplication by $i$ turns a point a quarter turn counterclockwise, and four quarter turns bring it home.

<Tip>
Four consecutive powers always add to zero: $i^n + i^{n+1} + i^{n+2} + i^{n+3} = i^n(1 + i - 1 - i) = 0$. Long sums of powers of $i$ mostly cancel.
</Tip>

## The conjugate and division

The **conjugate** of $z = a + bi$ is $\bar z = a - bi$, its mirror image across the real axis. The key fact:

$$
z\bar z = (a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 + b^2,
$$

a positive real number. That is exactly how you clear $i$ out of a denominator: multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom.

<Steps problem="Write $\dfrac{3 + 4i}{1 + 2i}$ in the form $a + bi$.">
<Step title="Multiply by the conjugate">
$\dfrac{(3 + 4i)(1 - 2i)}{(1 + 2i)(1 - 2i)}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Bottom">
$1^2 + 2^2 = 5$.
</Step>
<Step title="Top">
$3 - 6i + 4i - 8i^2 = 3 + 8 - 2i = 11 - 2i$.
</Step>
<Step title="Answer">
$\dfrac{11}{5} - \dfrac{2}{5}i$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Absolute value: distance from 0

$$
|a + bi| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \qquad |z|^2 = z\bar z.
$$

It's the Pythagorean Theorem in the complex plane. Absolute values multiply nicely: $|zw| = |z|\,|w|$ and $\left|\tfrac{z}{w}\right| = \tfrac{|z|}{|w|}$.

<Check choices={["$5$", "$7$", "$12$", "$13$", "$17$"]} answer={3} explain="$\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.">
What is $|5 - 12i|$?
</Check>

<Check choices={["$5$", "$25$", "$50$", "$125$", "$625$"]} answer={3} explain="$|3 + 4i| = 5$, and absolute values multiply, so $|(3 + 4i)^3| = 5^3 = 125$. No expanding needed.">
What is $|(3 + 4i)^3|$?
</Check>

## Matching parts

Two complex numbers are equal only when their real parts match **and** their imaginary parts match. One complex equation is really two real equations.

<Steps problem="Find real $a, b$ with $(a + bi)^2 = 3 + 4i$.">
<Step title="Expand">
$a^2 - b^2 + 2abi = 3 + 4i$.
</Step>
<Step title="Match">
$a^2 - b^2 = 3$ and $2ab = 4$, so $ab = 2$.
</Step>
<Step title="Solve">
Try $a = 2, b = 1$: $4 - 1 = 3$ ✓. So $a + bi = 2 + i$ (or $-2 - i$).
</Step>
</Steps>

<Trap>
$\sqrt{-4}\cdot\sqrt{-9}$ is **not** $\sqrt{36} = 6$. It's $2i \cdot 3i = 6i^2 = -6$. The rule $\sqrt{x}\sqrt{y} = \sqrt{xy}$ only holds when $x, y \ge 0$. Convert to $i$ first.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- $i^2 = -1$; complex numbers $a + bi$ are points $(a, b)$.
- Add parts separately; multiply by FOIL and replace $i^2$.
- Powers of $i$ cycle with period 4.
- Divide by multiplying by the conjugate; $z\bar z = |z|^2$.
- $|zw| = |z||w|$. Match real and imaginary parts to solve equations.
</Recap>
