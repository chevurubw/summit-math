title: Circles

A **circle** is every point at one fixed distance, the **radius** $r$, from a center. Circle problems are full of hidden equal lengths (every radius is the same!) and a few beautiful angle facts. Master the five ideas below and most AMC circle problems open up.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- find circumference, area, arc length and sector area,
- use the Inscribed Angle Theorem,
- use the facts about tangent lines,
- use Power of a Point for intersecting chords and secants.

## Vocabulary and the two basic formulas

- A **chord** joins two points on the circle. A **diameter** is a chord through the center, $d = 2r$.
- An **arc** is a piece of the circle. A **sector** is a pizza slice.
- A **tangent** line touches the circle at exactly one point.

$$
\text{circumference} = 2\pi r, \qquad \text{area} = \pi r^2.
$$

An arc or sector with central angle $\theta$ is the fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}$ of the whole:

$$
\text{arc length} = \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi r, \qquad \text{sector area} = \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot \pi r^2.
$$

## The Inscribed Angle Theorem

An **inscribed angle** has its vertex on the circle. It is always **half** of the central angle that cuts off the same arc. Move point $P$ around the circle and watch: the angle at $P$ never changes.

Two consequences you will use constantly:

- **An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle.** If $AB$ is a diameter, the central angle is $180^\circ$, so any $\angle APB = 90^\circ$.
- **Inscribed angles on the same arc are equal.** That creates equal angles out of nowhere, which leads to similar triangles.

## Tangent lines

<KeyIdea title="Two tangent facts">
1. A tangent line is **perpendicular** to the radius at the point where it touches. That makes a right triangle with the center.
2. The two tangent segments from an outside point to a circle are **equal** in length.
</KeyIdea>

<Steps problem="A point $P$ is 13 units from the center of a circle of radius 5. How long is a tangent segment from $P$ to the circle?">
<Step>
The radius to the point of tangency $T$ is perpendicular to $PT$, so triangle $OTP$ has a right angle at $T$.
</Step>
<Step>
$PT = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12$.
</Step>
</Steps>

The equal tangents fact explains why a circle inscribed in a triangle touches each side at a point whose distances to the corners are $s - a$, $s - b$, $s - c$, where $s$ is the semiperimeter. It also gives the **inradius** formula: area $= r \cdot s$.

## Power of a Point

When two chords cross inside a circle at point $P$, the products of the pieces match:

$$
PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD.
$$

The same is true outside the circle for two secants (measure from $P$ to the near point and to the far point), and for a tangent, where the tangent length gets squared: $PT^2 = PA \cdot PB$.

<Trap>
For secants from an outside point, use the **whole** distance to the far point, not the part inside the circle: $PA \cdot PB$ where $B$ is the far intersection.
</Trap>

<Tip>
In any circle problem, draw every radius you can to the important points. Equal radii make isosceles triangles, and radii to tangent points make right angles.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- $C = 2\pi r$, $A = \pi r^2$. Arcs and sectors take the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$.
- Inscribed angle $=$ half the central angle on the same arc. Semicircle angles are $90^\circ$.
- Tangent is perpendicular to the radius. Two tangents from a point are equal.
- Power of a Point: $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$, and $PT^2 = PA \cdot PB$ for a tangent.
- Draw the radii.
</Recap>
