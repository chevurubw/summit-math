---
title: Telescoping sums and products
---

Some sums look like they need a hundred additions, yet almost everything cancels and only a couple of terms survive. They're called **telescoping** because the sum collapses like a pocket telescope. The skill is rewriting each term as a **difference** of two pieces so that neighbors cancel.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- split fractions like $\tfrac{1}{k(k+1)}$ into differences,
- collapse sums and find what survives,
- telescope products by writing each factor as a fraction,
- recognize the square root and factorial versions.

## The classic: $\dfrac{1}{k(k+1)}$

Check this by combining the right side:

$$
\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k+1}.
$$

So the sum $\tfrac{1}{1 \cdot 2} + \tfrac{1}{2 \cdot 3} + \cdots + \tfrac{1}{5 \cdot 6}$ becomes a chain of differences. Press the button.

In general $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = 1 - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{n}{n+1}$.

What is $\dfrac{1}{1 \cdot 2} + \dfrac{1}{2 \cdot 3} + \cdots + \dfrac{1}{99 \cdot 100}$?
</Check>

## Gaps bigger than 1

When the two factors differ by $d$, there's a factor of $\tfrac{1}{d}$ in front:

$$
\frac{1}{k(k + d)} = \frac{1}{d}\left(\frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k + d}\right).
$$

Now the cancellation skips: each piece cancels with the term $d$ steps later, so $d$ pieces survive at each end.

<Steps problem="Evaluate $\dfrac{1}{1 \cdot 3} + \dfrac{1}{3 \cdot 5} + \dfrac{1}{5 \cdot 7} + \cdots + \dfrac{1}{19 \cdot 21}$.">
<Step title="Split">
Here consecutive factors differ by 2: $\dfrac{1}{k(k+2)} = \dfrac12\left(\dfrac1k - \dfrac{1}{k+2}\right)$ with $k = 1, 3, 5, \dots, 19$.
</Step>
<Step title="Telescope">
The odd reciprocals cancel in a chain: $\dfrac12\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{21}\right)$.
</Step>
<Step title="Answer">
$\dfrac12 \cdot \dfrac{20}{21} = \dfrac{10}{21}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Square roots

Rationalizing turns a nasty fraction into a difference:

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{k} + \sqrt{k+1}} = \frac{\sqrt{k+1} - \sqrt{k}}{(k + 1) - k} = \sqrt{k+1} - \sqrt{k}.
$$

<Check choices={["$9$", "$10$", "$\\sqrt{99}$", "$11$", "$99$"]} answer={0} explain="Each term is $\sqrt{k+1} - \sqrt{k}$, so the sum from $k = 1$ to $99$ telescopes to $\sqrt{100} - \sqrt{1} = 9$.">
What is $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{99}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k} + \sqrt{k+1}}$?
</Check>

## Telescoping products

In a product, write each factor as a fraction. Then **numerators cancel denominators**.

<Steps problem="Compute $\left(1 + \tfrac11\right)\left(1 + \tfrac12\right)\left(1 + \tfrac13\right)\cdots\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{99}\right)$.">
<Step title="Rewrite each factor">
$\dfrac21 \cdot \dfrac32 \cdot \dfrac43 \cdots \dfrac{100}{99}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Cancel">
Every numerator cancels the next denominator, leaving $\dfrac{100}{1} = 100$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{1}{10}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{9}{10}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{2}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{11}$"]} answer={0} explain="$\tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac23 \cdot \tfrac34 \cdots \tfrac{9}{10} = \tfrac{1}{10}$.">
What is $\left(1 - \tfrac12\right)\left(1 - \tfrac13\right)\cdots\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{10}\right)$?
</Check>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{1}{2}$", "$\\tfrac{5}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{4}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{10}{9}$", "$\\tfrac{2}{3}$"]} answer={1} explain="$1 - \tfrac{1}{k^2} = \tfrac{(k-1)(k+1)}{k \cdot k}$. Split the product into $\prod \tfrac{k-1}{k} = \tfrac{1}{9}$ and $\prod\tfrac{k+1}{k} = \tfrac{10}{2} = 5$, for $k = 2$ to $9$. The answer is $\tfrac{5}{9}$.">
What is $\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{2^2}\right)\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{3^2}\right)\cdots\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{9^2}\right)$?
</Check>

<KeyIdea>
When a sum or product has many terms with a regular pattern, try writing the general term as $f(k) - f(k+1)$ (for sums) or $\tfrac{g(k+1)}{g(k)}$ (for products). Then write out the first few and last few terms to see exactly what survives.
</KeyIdea>

<Trap>
Don't guess which terms survive. Write the first two and the last two terms explicitly. Off by one errors are the most common mistake here.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- Rewrite terms as differences so neighbors cancel; partial fractions help.
- Gap $d$: factor $\tfrac1d$ out, and $d$ pieces survive at each end.
- Square root sums: rationalize to get $\sqrt{k+1} - \sqrt{k}$.
- Products: write factors as fractions; numerators cancel denominators.
</Recap>
