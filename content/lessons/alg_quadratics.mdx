title: Quadratics
---

A **quadratic** is an expression like $ax^2 + bx + c$ with $a \ne 0$. Its graph is a **parabola**, a U shape that opens up when $a > 0$ and down when $a < 0$. Quadratics show up everywhere: areas, projectiles, maximum profit, and as the hidden step inside harder problems.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- read a parabola's shape, vertex and roots from its equation,
- solve any quadratic three ways,
- predict how many real roots there are without solving,
- use Vieta's formulas to get the sum and product of the roots instantly.

## The parabola, live

In **vertex form**, $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$, the vertex is $(h, k)$. Play with all three numbers.

Notice that when the vertex is below the $x$ axis and the parabola opens up, there are two roots. Lift the vertex above the axis and the roots vanish. Exactly touching gives one root.

## Three ways to solve $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$

**1. Factor** (fastest when it works). $x^2 - 6x + 8 = (x - 2)(x - 4) = 0$, so $x = 2$ or $x = 4$.

**2. Complete the square** (always works, and it's where the formula comes from).

<Steps problem="Solve $x^2 + 6x - 7 = 0$ by completing the square.">
<Step>
Move the constant: $x^2 + 6x = 7$.
</Step>
<Step>
Take half of the $x$ coefficient, square it ($3^2 = 9$), and add to both sides: $x^2 + 6x + 9 = 16$.
</Step>
<Step>
The left side is a perfect square: $(x + 3)^2 = 16$, so $x + 3 = \pm 4$.
</Step>
<Step>
$x = 1$ or $x = -7$.
</Step>
</Steps>

**3. The quadratic formula** (always works).

<Formula title="Quadratic formula">

$$
x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
$$

</Formula>

For $x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$: $x = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 4}}{2} = \dfrac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{3}$.

<Check choices={["$-4$", "$-2$", "$2$", "$4$", "$8$"]} answer={3} explain="$x^2 - 6x + 8 = (x - 2)(x - 4)$, so the roots are 2 and 4. The larger is 4.">
What is the larger root of $x^2 - 6x + 8 = 0$?
</Check>

## The discriminant counts the roots

The part under the square root, $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$, is called the **discriminant**.

| $b^2 - 4ac$ | Real roots | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| positive | two | parabola crosses the axis twice |
| zero | one (a double root) | vertex sits on the axis |
| negative | none | parabola misses the axis |

<Check choices={["$3$", "$6$", "$9$", "$12$", "$18$"]} answer={1} explain="Exactly one root means $b^2 - 4ac = 0$: $k^2 - 36 = 0$, so $k = 6$ (we want the positive value). Then $x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2$.">
For what positive value of $k$ does $x^2 + kx + 9 = 0$ have exactly one real solution?
</Check>

## Maximum and minimum: the vertex

The vertex is the highest or lowest point. For $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ it sits at

$$
x = -\frac{b}{2a}.
$$

Plug that $x$ back in to get the extreme value. This answers every "largest area" or "highest point" question.

<Steps problem="A farmer has 40 m of fence to make a rectangle. What is the largest possible area?">
<Step title="Write the area as a quadratic">
If one side is $x$, the other is $20 - x$ (half the perimeter is 20). Area $A = x(20 - x) = -x^2 + 20x$.
</Step>
<Step title="Find the vertex">
$x = -\dfrac{20}{2(-1)} = 10$. The rectangle is a $10 \times 10$ square.
</Step>
<Step title="Evaluate">
$A = 10 \cdot 10 = 100$ square meters.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$6$", "$8$", "$9$", "$10$", "$12$"]} answer={3} explain="The vertex is at $x = -\dfrac{6}{2(-1)} = 3$. Then $y = -9 + 18 + 1 = 10$.">
What is the maximum value of $y = -x^2 + 6x + 1$?
</Check>

## Vieta's formulas: sum and product for free

If $r$ and $s$ are the roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, then

$$
r + s = -\frac{b}{a}, \qquad rs = \frac{c}{a}.
$$

**Why:** $a(x - r)(x - s) = ax^2 - a(r + s)x + ars$. Match coefficients with $ax^2 + bx + c$.

Vieta is a huge time saver: many problems ask about $r + s$, $rs$, $r^2 + s^2$, or $\tfrac{1}{r} + \tfrac{1}{s}$, and you never need the roots themselves.

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{3}{7}$", "$\\tfrac{3}{2}$", "$\\tfrac{7}{3}$", "$\\tfrac{7}{2}$", "$7$"]} answer={2} explain="$r + s = \tfrac{7}{2}$ and $rs = \tfrac{3}{2}$. Then $\tfrac{1}{r} + \tfrac{1}{s} = \tfrac{r + s}{rs} = \tfrac{7/2}{3/2} = \tfrac{7}{3}$.">
If $r$ and $s$ are the roots of $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$, what is $\dfrac{1}{r} + \dfrac{1}{s}$?
</Check>

<Trap>
Sum of roots is $-\tfrac{b}{a}$, with a minus sign. The product $\tfrac{c}{a}$ has no minus sign. Mixing these up is the most common Vieta mistake.
</Trap>

<Tip>
If the problem gives a quadratic and asks about its roots, ask yourself: "Can I write what they want in terms of $r + s$ and $rs$?" If yes, Vieta answers it in two lines.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Vertex form $a(x - h)^2 + k$ shows the vertex $(h, k)$; $a$ sets the direction and width.
- Solve by factoring, completing the square, or $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.
- $b^2 - 4ac$: positive two roots, zero one root, negative none.
- Extreme value at $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$.
- Vieta: $r + s = -\frac{b}{a}$, $rs = \frac{c}{a}$.
</Recap>
