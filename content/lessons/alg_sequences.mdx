---
title: Arithmetic and geometric sequences
---

A **sequence** is a list of numbers in order. Two kinds dominate the AMC. In an **arithmetic** sequence you add the same number each time: $3, 7, 11, 15, \ldots$ In a **geometric** sequence you multiply by the same number each time: $2, 6, 18, 54, \ldots$

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- find any term of either kind of sequence,
- count how many terms are in a list,
- add up an arithmetic sequence with Gauss's pairing trick,
- add up a geometric sequence with one formula.

## Arithmetic sequences: add the same amount

The amount you add is the **common difference** $d$. To reach term $n$ you start at $a_1$ and take $n - 1$ steps of size $d$:

$$
a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)d.
$$

<SequenceBars kind="arithmetic" a={3} d={4} terms={8} caption="Bars grow by the same amount each step. Switch to running sums to see the totals climb faster and faster." />

<Trap title="The fence post problem">
It's $n - 1$ steps, not $n$, because the first term needs no step. The 10th term of $3, 7, 11, \ldots$ is $3 + 9 \cdot 4 = 39$, not $3 + 10 \cdot 4$.
</Trap>

What is the 20th term of $3, 7, 11, 15, \ldots$?
</Check>

### How many terms?

For a list that runs from $\text{first}$ to $\text{last}$ by steps of $d$:

$$
\text{number of terms} = \frac{\text{last} - \text{first}}{d} + 1.
$$

For $5, 9, 13, \ldots, 101$: $\dfrac{101 - 5}{4} + 1 = 25$ terms.

### Two terms given? Find $d$ from the gap

<Steps problem="An arithmetic sequence has $a_5 = 17$ and $a_9 = 29$. Find $a_{20}$.">
<Step>
From term 5 to term 9 is 4 steps, and the value rises by $29 - 17 = 12$. So $d = 3$.
</Step>
<Step>
From term 9 to term 20 is 11 more steps: $a_{20} = 29 + 11 \cdot 3 = 62$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Adding an arithmetic sequence: Gauss's trick

To add $1 + 2 + \cdots + 100$, write the sum forwards and backwards and add them up:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 1 + 2 + \cdots + 100 \\
S &= 100 + 99 + \cdots + 1 \\
2S &= 101 + 101 + \cdots + 101 = 100 \cdot 101
\end{aligned}
$$

So $S = 5050$. Every arithmetic sum works the same way:

<Formula>

$$
\text{sum} = \frac{\text{number of terms} \times (\text{first} + \text{last})}{2}
$$

It's just the count times the average of the first and last term.
</Formula>

</Check>

## Geometric sequences: multiply by the same amount

The number you multiply by is the **common ratio** $r$. Term $n$ takes $n - 1$ multiplications:

$$
a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{\,n - 1}.
$$

</Check>

### Adding a geometric sequence

Multiply the sum by $r$ and subtract. Almost everything cancels:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= a + ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1} \\
rS &= \phantom{a + {}} ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1} + ar^n \\
S - rS &= a - ar^n
\end{aligned}
$$

<Formula>

$$
a + ar + \cdots + ar^{n-1} = a \cdot \frac{r^n - 1}{r - 1} \qquad (r \ne 1)
$$

</Formula>

For example $1 + 2 + 4 + \cdots + 2^9 = \dfrac{2^{10} - 1}{2 - 1} = 1023$.

## Means in the middle

If three numbers $a, b, c$ form a sequence:

- **arithmetic**: the middle is the average, $b = \dfrac{a + c}{2}$, or $2b = a + c$;
- **geometric**: the middle squared is the product of the ends, $b^2 = ac$.

<Check choices={["$-6$", "$-3$", "$3$", "$6$", "$9$"]} answer={3} explain="Geometric means $(x)^2 = 4 \cdot 9 = 36$, so $x = \pm 6$. The positive value is 6.">
The numbers $4, x, 9$ form a geometric sequence with $x > 0$. What is $x$?
</Check>

<Tip>
If a problem says three numbers are "in arithmetic progression", name them $a - d$, $a$, $a + d$. The symmetry makes sums very clean: they add to $3a$.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Arithmetic: $a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)d$. Geometric: $a_n = a_1 r^{n - 1}$.
- Count terms with $\frac{\text{last} - \text{first}}{d} + 1$.
- Arithmetic sum: count times the average of first and last.
- Geometric sum: $a \cdot \frac{r^n - 1}{r - 1}$.
- Middle terms: $2b = a + c$ (arithmetic), $b^2 = ac$ (geometric).
</Recap>
