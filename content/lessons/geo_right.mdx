title: Right triangles
---

A **right triangle** has one $90^\circ$ angle. The two sides that form the right angle are the **legs**, and the long side across from it is the **hypotenuse**. Right triangles are the single most useful shape on the AMC: whenever you need a length, your first thought should be "can I make a right triangle?"

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- use the Pythagorean Theorem in both directions,
- recognize the common Pythagorean triples on sight,
- use the $45$-$45$-$90$ and $30$-$60$-$90$ triangles without computing,
- find the altitude to the hypotenuse.

## The Pythagorean Theorem

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are the legs and $c$ is the hypotenuse. Here is what it **means**: the square built on the hypotenuse has exactly the same area as the two squares on the legs put together. Change the legs and compare the areas.

<Trap>
$c$ is always the side **across from the right angle**, the longest side. If you are given the hypotenuse and one leg, subtract: $b = \sqrt{c^2 - a^2}$.
</Trap>

## Pythagorean triples: memorize these

Whole number solutions come up constantly. Know these, and know that **any multiple** of a triple is also a triple ($6, 8, 10$ and $9, 12, 15$ come from $3, 4, 5$).

| Triple | Multiples you'll see |
| --- | --- |
| $3, 4, 5$ | $6, 8, 10$ and $9, 12, 15$ and $15, 20, 25$ |
| $5, 12, 13$ | $10, 24, 26$ |
| $8, 15, 17$ | |
| $7, 24, 25$ | |
| $20, 21, 29$ | |

<Tip>
Before squaring big numbers, divide out a common factor. For legs $45$ and $60$, notice $45 : 60 = 3 : 4$ with factor 15, so the hypotenuse is $5 \cdot 15 = 75$. No squaring needed.
</Tip>

## The two special right triangles

**45-45-90** (half a square). The legs are equal and the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{2}$ times a leg:

$$
x : x : x\sqrt{2}.
$$

**30-60-90** (half an equilateral triangle). The short leg is across from $30^\circ$:

$$
x : x\sqrt{3} : 2x.
$$

### Two quick consequences

- A square with side $s$ has diagonal $s\sqrt{2}$.
- An equilateral triangle with side $s$ has height $\dfrac{s\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and area $\dfrac{s^2\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

## The altitude to the hypotenuse

Drop a perpendicular from the right angle to the hypotenuse. Computing the area two ways gives its length:

$$
\frac{1}{2}ab = \frac{1}{2}c\,h \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h = \frac{ab}{c}.
$$

<Steps problem="A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. How long is the altitude to the hypotenuse?">
<Step>
The hypotenuse is 10 (a $3, 4, 5$ triple times 2).
</Step>
<Step>
$h = \dfrac{6 \cdot 8}{10} = 4.8$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea title="Make your own right triangle">
Distances between points, heights of isosceles triangles, chords in circles, diagonals of boxes: almost all are found by drawing one helpful perpendicular and using $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.
</KeyIdea>

<Recap>
- $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ with $c$ the hypotenuse across from the right angle.
- Triples: $3, 4, 5$; $5, 12, 13$; $8, 15, 17$; $7, 24, 25$; $20, 21, 29$; and their multiples.
- $45$-$45$-$90$: $x : x : x\sqrt{2}$. $30$-$60$-$90$: $x : x\sqrt{3} : 2x$.
- Altitude to the hypotenuse: $\frac{ab}{c}$.
- Need a length? Build a right triangle.
</Recap>
