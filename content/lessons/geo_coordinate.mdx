---
title: Coordinate geometry
---

**Coordinate geometry** puts shapes on a grid so you can measure them with algebra. Every point is a pair $(x, y)$, and lengths, slopes and areas become arithmetic. When a geometry problem feels stuck, dropping it onto coordinates is often the reliable way through.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- find distances and midpoints,
- write the equation of a line from two facts about it,
- find the distance from a point to a line,
- reflect points and pick smart coordinates for a problem.

## Distance is Pythagoras in disguise

To get from $(x_1, y_1)$ to $(x_2, y_2)$, walk across $x_2 - x_1$ and up $y_2 - y_1$. Those are the legs of a right triangle, and the distance is the hypotenuse.

<Formula>

$$
d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}, \qquad \text{midpoint} = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right).
$$

</Formula>

What is the distance between $(1, 2)$ and $(7, 10)$?
</Check>

## Lines

Slope $m = \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$. Two common forms of a line:

- **slope intercept**: $y = mx + b$,
- **point slope**: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$, perfect when you know a point and the slope.

Parallel lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular slopes multiply to $-1$.

<Steps problem="Find the line through $(2, 3)$ perpendicular to $y = 2x + 1$. Where does it cross the $y$ axis?">
<Step title="Perpendicular slope">
The negative reciprocal of $2$ is $-\tfrac{1}{2}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Point slope form">
$y - 3 = -\tfrac{1}{2}(x - 2)$, so $y = -\tfrac{1}{2}x + 4$.
</Step>
<Step title="The intercept">
It crosses the $y$ axis at $(0, 4)$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$(2, 2)$", "$(2, 3)$", "$(4, 4)$", "$(5, 3)$", "$(1, 2)$"]} answer={0} explain="Average the coordinates: $\left(\tfrac{-3 + 7}{2}, \tfrac{5 + (-1)}{2}\right) = (2, 2)$.">
What is the midpoint of $(-3, 5)$ and $(7, -1)$?
</Check>

## Distance from a point to a line

<Formula>
The distance from $(x_0, y_0)$ to the line $ax + by + c = 0$ is

$$
\frac{|ax_0 + by_0 + c|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}.
$$

</Formula>

<Check choices={["$3$", "$4$", "$5$", "$\\tfrac{20}{7}$", "$20$"]} answer={1} explain="Write the line as $3x + 4y - 20 = 0$. From $(0, 0)$: $\tfrac{|0 + 0 - 20|}{\sqrt{9 + 16}} = \tfrac{20}{5} = 4$.">
How far is the origin from the line $3x + 4y = 20$?
</Check>

## Reflections

| Reflect $(x, y)$ across | Result |
| --- | --- |
| the $x$ axis | $(x, -y)$ |
| the $y$ axis | $(-x, y)$ |
| the line $y = x$ | $(y, x)$ |
| the origin | $(-x, -y)$ |

<KeyIdea title="The shortest path trick">
To find the shortest path from $A$ to a line and then to $B$ (both on the same side), reflect $B$ across the line. The shortest path is the straight segment from $A$ to the reflected point. This turns a "bounce" problem into a single distance.
</KeyIdea>

<Steps problem="What is the shortest path from $(0, 3)$ to a point on the $x$ axis and then to $(8, 3)$?">
<Step>
Reflect $(8, 3)$ across the $x$ axis to $(8, -3)$.
</Step>
<Step>
The straight line from $(0, 3)$ to $(8, -3)$ has length $\sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Choosing coordinates

When a problem gives a shape but no coordinates, **place it yourself** to make the numbers easy: put a right angle at the origin, a side along an axis, or a center of symmetry at $(0, 0)$. A square of side 4 might get corners $(0, 0)$, $(4, 0)$, $(4, 4)$, $(0, 4)$.

<Tip>
On the AMC, coordinates are a reliable fallback. They may take a few more lines than a clever synthetic trick, but they almost never fail. If you don't see the trick in a minute, set up coordinates.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Distance is Pythagoras on the changes in $x$ and $y$. Midpoint is the average.
- Slope is rise over run; use point slope form to write a line quickly.
- Point to line distance: $\frac{|ax_0 + by_0 + c|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}$.
- Reflections swap or negate coordinates. Reflecting turns bounce paths into straight lines.
- Place shapes on axes to make coordinates easy.
</Recap>
