title: Probability
---

**Probability** measures how likely something is, as a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). When all outcomes are equally likely, it's simply a counting problem:

$$
P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{number of outcomes you want}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}.
$$

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- list equally likely outcomes correctly (the most important step),
- use the complement for "at least one",
- multiply probabilities of independent events,
- handle drawing without replacement.

## Two dice: 36 outcomes, not 11

Rolling two dice, think of one as red and one as blue. Each has 6 faces, so there are $6 \times 6 = 36$ **equally likely** outcomes. The sums 2 through 12 are **not** equally likely: a sum of 7 happens 6 ways, a sum of 2 only one way. Click a rule to highlight its outcomes.

Always count outcomes that are **equally likely**. "The sum is 2, 3, ..., 12, so $P(7) = \tfrac{1}{11}$" is wrong because those 11 sums are not equally likely.
</Trap>

## The complement

$$
P(\text{not } E) = 1 - P(E).
$$

For "at least one", compute the probability of **none** and subtract from 1.

<Steps problem="Three fair coins are flipped. What is the probability of at least one head?">
<Step>
The opposite is no heads at all: TTT. Probability $\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \tfrac{1}{8}$.
</Step>
<Step>
$1 - \tfrac{1}{8} = \tfrac{7}{8}$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{1}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{2}$", "$\\tfrac{2}{3}$", "$\\tfrac{3}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{5}{6}$"]} answer={3} explain="The product is odd only when both dice are odd: $\tfrac{3}{6} \cdot \tfrac{3}{6} = \tfrac{1}{4}$. So the product is even with probability $1 - \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{3}{4}$. Check it on the grid above.">
Two dice are rolled. What is the probability that the product of the numbers is even?
</Check>

## Independent events multiply

Two events are **independent** if one happening doesn't change the chance of the other (separate coin flips, separate dice). Then

$$
P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \cdot P(B).
$$

Rolling a 6 and then flipping heads: $\tfrac{1}{6} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{12}$.

## Without replacement: the numbers change

When you draw marbles and don't put them back, each draw changes what's left. Multiply step by step with the **updated** counts.

<Steps problem="A bag has 3 red and 2 blue marbles. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability both are red?">
<Step title="First draw">
$P(\text{red}) = \tfrac{3}{5}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Second draw, given the first was red">
Now 2 red out of 4: $\tfrac{2}{4}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Multiply">
$\tfrac{3}{5} \cdot \tfrac{2}{4} = \tfrac{6}{20} = \tfrac{3}{10}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Check by counting">
Pairs of marbles: $\binom{5}{2} = 10$. Red pairs: $\binom{3}{2} = 3$. So $\tfrac{3}{10}$ ✓.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea title="Two ways, same answer">
You can either multiply probabilities step by step, or count favorable groups over total groups with combinations. Use whichever is easier, and use the other to check.
</KeyIdea>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{1}{221}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{169}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{26}$", "$\\tfrac{1}{13}$", "$\\tfrac{4}{51}$"]} answer={0} explain="First card an ace: $\tfrac{4}{52}$. <Recap>
- Equally likely outcomes: probability $=$ favorable over total.
- Two dice have 36 outcomes; sums are not equally likely.
- "At least one": $1 - P(\text{none})$.
- Independent events multiply.
- Without replacement: update the counts after each draw, or count with combinations.
</Recap>
