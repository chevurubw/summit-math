---
title: Change of base and digits
---

Logs in different bases are all the same curve, just stretched. The **change of base formula** lets you rewrite any log in any base you like, and it powers a family of AMC tricks: chains of logs that collapse, reciprocal logs, and counting the digits of enormous numbers.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- convert a log to any base,
- simplify products like $\log_2 3 \cdot \log_3 4 \cdots$,
- count the digits of a huge power,
- trap a log between two integers.

## The formula

$$
\log_b x = \frac{\log_c x}{\log_c b} \quad \text{for any base } c.
$$

**Why:** let $y = \log_b x$, so $b^y = x$. Take $\log_c$ of both sides: $y\log_c b = \log_c x$.

Every log curve is the same shape scaled by $\tfrac{1}{\log b}$:

## Consequences you'll use constantly

<Formula>

$$
\log_b a = \frac{1}{\log_a b}, \qquad \log_a b \cdot \log_b c = \log_a c, \qquad \log_{b^n} x^m = \frac{m}{n}\log_b x.
$$

</Formula>

<Steps problem="Evaluate $\log_2 3 \cdot \log_3 4 \cdot \log_4 5 \cdot \log_5 6 \cdot \log_6 7 \cdot \log_7 8$.">
<Step>
Write each as a ratio of natural logs: $\dfrac{\ln 3}{\ln 2} \cdot \dfrac{\ln 4}{\ln 3} \cdots \dfrac{\ln 8}{\ln 7}$.
</Step>
<Step>
Everything cancels except $\dfrac{\ln 8}{\ln 2} = \log_2 8 = 3$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{2}{3}$", "$\\tfrac{3}{4}$", "$\\tfrac{4}{3}$", "$\\tfrac{3}{2}$", "$2$"]} answer={3} explain="$\log_4 8 = \tfrac{\log_2 8}{\log_2 4} = \tfrac{3}{2}$.">
What is $\log_4 8$?
</Check>

If $\log_2 x = 6$, what is $\log_8 x$?
</Check>

## Counting digits

A positive integer $N$ has $d$ digits exactly when $10^{d-1} \le N < 10^d$, that is, when $d - 1 \le \log N < d$. So

$$
\text{number of digits of } N = \lfloor \log_{10} N \rfloor + 1.
$$

<Steps problem="Using $\log 2 \approx 0.30103$, how many digits does $2^{100}$ have?">
<Step>
$\log 2^{100} = 100 \log 2 \approx 30.103$.
</Step>
<Step>
$\lfloor 30.103 \rfloor + 1 = 31$ digits.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$5$", "$6$", "$7$", "$8$", "$20$"]} answer={2} explain="$20 \log 2 \approx 6.02$, so $2^{20}$ has $\lfloor 6.02 \rfloor + 1 = 7$ digits. (Indeed $2^{20} = 1{,}048{,}576$.)">
Using $\log 2 \approx 0.301$, how many digits does $2^{20}$ have?
</Check>

## Trapping a log between integers

To estimate $\log_2 100$, find powers of 2 on either side: $2^6 = 64 < 100 < 128 = 2^7$. So $6 < \log_2 100 < 7$. AMC answer choices are often far enough apart that this is all you need.

<Tip>
Memorize $\log 2 \approx 0.301$ and $\log 3 \approx 0.477$. Then $\log 4, \log 5 = 1 - \log 2, \log 6, \log 8, \log 9$ all follow from the log rules.
</Tip>

<Trap>
$\dfrac{\log 8}{\log 2}$ is $\log_2 8 = 3$, **not** $\log 8 - \log 2 = \log 4$.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- $\log_b x = \frac{\log_c x}{\log_c b}$ for any base $c$.
- $\log_b a = \frac{1}{\log_a b}$; chains $\log_a b \cdot \log_b c = \log_a c$ telescope.
- Digits of $N$: $\lfloor \log_{10} N \rfloor + 1$.
- Bound logs by the powers around the number.
</Recap>
