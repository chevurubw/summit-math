---
title: Law of Sines and Cosines
---

Right triangle trig only works when there's a right angle. The **Law of Cosines** and the **Law of Sines** work in **every** triangle. With them you can find any missing side or angle once you know three facts about a triangle (as long as one of them is a side).

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- use the Law of Cosines to find a side or an angle,
- use the Law of Sines, and connect it to the circumradius,
- find area with $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ and with Heron's formula,
- pick the right tool for the information you have.

**Notation:** in triangle $ABC$, side $a$ is across from angle $A$, side $b$ across from $B$, side $c$ across from $C$.

## Law of Cosines

$$
c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C
$$

It's the Pythagorean Theorem with a correction term. When $C = 90^\circ$, $\cos C = 0$ and it becomes $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$. Open the angle wider and $c$ grows; close it and $c$ shrinks.

**Finding an angle:** rearrange to $\cos C = \dfrac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$. For sides $5, 7, 8$, the angle across from 7 has $\cos = \tfrac{25 + 64 - 49}{80} = \tfrac{1}{2}$, so it's $60^\circ$.

<Tip>
The sign of $a^2 + b^2 - c^2$ tells you the type of angle $C$: positive means acute, zero means right, negative means obtuse. Quick way to classify a triangle.
</Tip>

## Law of Sines

$$
\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} = 2R
$$

where $R$ is the radius of the circle through all three vertices (the **circumradius**). Use it when you know an angle and the side across from it.

<Steps problem="In triangle $ABC$, $A = 30^\circ$, $B = 45^\circ$, and $a = 6$. Find $b$.">
<Step>
$\dfrac{6}{\sin 30^\circ} = \dfrac{b}{\sin 45^\circ}$.
</Step>
<Step>
$\dfrac{6}{1/2} = 12$, so $b = 12 \sin 45^\circ = 6\sqrt{2}$.
</Step>
<Step title="Bonus">
$2R = 12$, so the circumradius is $6$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$\\tfrac{5}{2}$", "$5$", "$5\\sqrt{3}$", "$10$", "$\\tfrac{10}{\\sqrt{3}}$"]} answer={1} explain="$2R = \tfrac{a}{\sin A} = \tfrac{5}{1/2} = 10$, so $R = 5$.">
A triangle has an angle of $30^\circ$ across from a side of length 5. What is its circumradius?
</Check>

## Area formulas

<Formula>

$$
\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C
$$

Two sides and the angle between them. And when you know all three sides, **Heron's formula**: with $s = \tfrac{a + b + c}{2}$,

$$
\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}.
$$

</Formula>

<Steps problem="Find the area of the triangle with sides 13, 14, 15.">
<Step>
$s = \tfrac{13 + 14 + 15}{2} = 21$.
</Step>
<Step>
Area $= \sqrt{21 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6} = \sqrt{7056} = 84$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Check choices={["$15$", "$15\\sqrt{3}$", "$30$", "$30\\sqrt{3}$", "$60$"]} answer={0} explain="$\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot 10 \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 30 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = 15$.">
What is the area of a triangle with sides 6 and 10 and a $30^\circ$ angle between them?
</Check>

## Which tool when?

| You know | Use |
| --- | --- |
| two sides and the angle between them | Law of Cosines (and $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ for area) |
| all three sides | Law of Cosines for angles, Heron for area |
| two angles and any side | Law of Sines |
| a side and the angle across from it | Law of Sines, and that gives $R$ too |

<Trap>
Knowing two sides and an angle **not** between them is the ambiguous case: there can be zero, one or two triangles. Draw it before trusting a single answer.
</Trap>

<Recap>
- Law of Cosines: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$.
- Law of Sines: $\frac{a}{\sin A} = 2R$ for every side.
- Area: $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, or Heron with the semiperimeter.
- Choose the law by what you know; beware the ambiguous case.
</Recap>
