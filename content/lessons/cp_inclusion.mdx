---
title: Inclusion and exclusion
---

When groups overlap, adding their sizes counts the overlap twice. The **Principle of Inclusion and Exclusion** (PIE) fixes this: add the groups, subtract the overlaps, add back the triple overlap, and so on. A Venn diagram makes every step visible.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- count the union of two or three overlapping sets,
- count "neither" and "exactly one",
- count numbers divisible by at least one of several numbers,
- fill in a Venn diagram from the inside out.

## Two sets

In a class of 30, 18 play soccer, 15 play basketball, and 7 play both. Adding $18 + 15 = 33$ counts the 7 two sport players twice, so

$$
|S \cup B| = |S| + |B| - |S \cap B| = 18 + 15 - 7 = 26.
$$

Then $30 - 26 = 4$ students play neither. Click the buttons to see each piece.

## Divisibility: the classic use

<Steps problem="How many integers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 2 or 3?">
<Step title="Each set">
Divisible by 2: $\lfloor 100/2 \rfloor = 50$. By 3: $\lfloor 100/3 \rfloor = 33$.
</Step>
<Step title="The overlap">
Divisible by both 2 and 3 means divisible by 6: $\lfloor 100/6 \rfloor = 16$.
</Step>
<Step title="Combine">
$50 + 33 - 16 = 67$.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Trap>
The overlap of "divisible by $a$" and "divisible by $b$" is "divisible by $\text{lcm}(a, b)$", not $ab$. Divisible by 4 and by 6 means divisible by 12, not 24.
</Trap>

## Three sets

$$
|A \cup B \cup C| = |A| + |B| + |C| - |A \cap B| - |B \cap C| - |A \cap C| + |A \cap B \cap C|
$$

Why add the triple overlap back? Someone in all three sets is added 3 times, then subtracted 3 times (once in each pair), leaving 0. Adding $|A \cap B \cap C|$ counts them once.

<Steps problem="Using the numbers above, fill the Venn diagram.">
<Step title="Start in the center">
All three: 3.
</Step>
<Step title="Pairs only">
$A$ and $B$ only: $7 - 3 = 4$. $B$ and $C$ only: $5 - 3 = 2$. $A$ and $C$ only: $6 - 3 = 3$.
</Step>
<Step title="Singles only">
$A$ only: $20 - 4 - 3 - 3 = 10$. $B$ only: $18 - 4 - 2 - 3 = 9$. $C$ only: $15 - 3 - 2 - 3 = 7$.
</Step>
<Step title="Add every region">
$10 + 9 + 7 + 4 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 38$, matching the formula.
</Step>
</Steps>

How many integers from 1 to 60 are divisible by none of 2, 3 and 5?

<KeyIdea>
When a problem gives overlap information, **fill the Venn diagram from the innermost region outward**. Then any question (exactly one, exactly two, neither) is just adding the right regions.
</KeyIdea>

<Tip>
"How many are in **neither**" and "how many are in **exactly one**" are the two most common twists. Draw the diagram; don't try to remember separate formulas.
</Tip>

<Recap>
- Two sets: $|A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B|$.
- Three sets: add singles, subtract pairs, add the triple.
- "Neither" = total minus union.
- Divisible by $a$ and $b$ means divisible by $\text{lcm}(a, b)$.
- Fill Venn diagrams from the center out.
</Recap>
